Management & Maintenance

Jin-in-Seoul preserves literary works and their translations as independent HTML files. Its management documents record the principles of preservation, editing, translation, publication and technical maintenance, so that the works and the operation of the site remain independent of any particular software or administrator.

This public edition presents the site's preservation, editorial, translation and maintenance principles. The approved Korean originals and complete bilingual management documents, including technical work history, remain in the repository. This page is reviewed separately and does not change automatically with internal maintenance.

ABC — Site Operation and Maintenance Regulations

{% capture public_management_snapshot %} ## 1. Purpose These regulations aim to preserve the literary identity of Jin-in-Seoul, ensure the stable publication and preservation of works, maintain consistency in site operation, and secure technical stability. ## 2. Site Identity Jin-in-Seoul is a personal literary website presenting novels, short stories, children's books, essays, haiku, music and tales from premodern Korea. - English-speaking readers are given priority. The usual multilingual display order is English · 日本語 · Français · 한국어. Existing conventions for individual collections, including haiku and music, follow their respective detailed regulations and editorial principles. - Jin-in-Seoul is the pen name used for the creation and publication of works. Public author information follows the approved pen name. ## 3. Operating Environment - Official site: https://jin-in-seoul.com/ - Publishing environment: GitHub Pages / Jekyll - Existing work URLs and publication structures are maintained on a continuing basis. ## 4. Management Regulations These regulations establish the fundamental principles of site operation. Understanding the management system begins with this document and README. The following documents govern the individual areas. | Document | Scope | | --- | --- | | [README.md](README.md) | Management system and document index | | [CSS-MAINTENANCE.md](CSS-MAINTENANCE.md) | Design and CSS | | [SEO-MAINTENANCE.md](SEO-MAINTENANCE.md) | Search engine optimisation and audits | | [PUBLICATION-LOG-POLICY.md](PUBLICATION-LOG-POLICY.md) | Publication records | | [ASSET-MAINTENANCE.md](ASSET-MAINTENANCE.md) | Digital assets | | [PREMODERN-KOREAN-FICTION-TRANSLATION-GUIDE.md](PREMODERN-KOREAN-FICTION-TRANSLATION-GUIDE.md) | Shared translation principles and glossary for premodern Korean fiction | - Both these regulations and the relevant detailed regulations apply to site management. Historical work records are distinguished from the current operating state, which is determined by the actual latest files on main. Conflicts between regulations require clarification and approval before changes are made. ## 5. Preservation and Editing of Works - Literary texts, titles, translations, paragraph structures and distinctive typography are respected and preserved as the results of creative and editorial work. - Literary and editorial changes are subject to the author's decisions. Technical maintenance is distinguished from literary editing. Existing content or publication formats are not changed merely to improve management efficiency. - Foreign-language translations follow the approved Korean source text. For premodern Korean fiction, the established modern Korean edition, its source edition and the applicable translation regulations are followed. ## 6. Design and Reader Experience - The site seeks simple and consistent literary typography. - Menus, links, buttons and other visual elements are used only when they have a clear function and are necessary. - Multilingual publication aims to provide works to readers in their own languages. Language selection is centred on work listings; additional language-switching functions within reading pages require separate approval. - Established typography and design appropriate to each work are maintained. In particular, the diary typography of Sweater and its related CSS are protected under the CSS maintenance regulations. ## 7. Technical Maintenance Technical maintenance aims to improve the stability of the existing publishing system, accuracy of work and management efficiency. - New management functions are arranged independently of existing HTML, CSS and publication structures wherever possible. Work scope is established in advance, and changes outside the approved scope require separate review and approval. Requests for review or opinions are distinguished from approval to make changes. ## 8. Management Tools The site's management tools support publication planning, checking proposed changes and reporting deployment status. They are maintained separately from published literary works and operate as read-only aids. - Tools do not have independent authority to alter or publish literary works. Proposals require the author's review and approval. - Technical tools are replaceable; the published works and the reader experience take priority. ## 9. Publication of Works New works and translations require the following checks as applicable: - Work text and title - Work listings and relevant homepage entries - Search, language and sharing information - Publication Log - Publication records and search information reflect works and language editions actually published. Publication dates, language order and preservation of existing records follow the Publication Log policy. - Decisions concerning digital assets, including the suspension of PDF and EPUB publication, follow the asset maintenance regulations. ## 10. Change Control and Verification - Every authorized change starts from the latest verified site state and is reviewed against the approved scope. - The effect of each change and its deployment are independently verified. - Technical checks must not be represented as proof of matters they have not verified. ## 11. Recovery and History Preservation - When technical errors occur, their scope and cause are identified and the previous working state is restored to the extent necessary. - Unrelated work and existing publication records are preserved during recovery. Technical change history remains traceable in the internal repository. ## 12. Maintenance and Revision of Regulations - These regulations and the detailed regulations for individual areas may be revised according to the site's operating environment and publishing system. - Revisions require the author's approval and maintain consistency with existing regulations.

The Korean edition is the authoritative original of these regulations. This English public edition presents the approved operating principles. Full bilingual management documents and technical work history are preserved separately in the repository.

Asset Maintenance

{% capture public_management_snapshot %} ## 1. Purpose This document defines the management principles for images, audio files, PDF editions, and EPUB editions published on Jin-in-Seoul.com. Objectives: - Preservation of the existing repository structure and stable published URLs. - Control of storage use and page loading performance. - Prevention of accidental replacement or deletion of published assets. - Separation of original materials from web-ready files. - Consistent procedures for future additions. - Existing published assets must not be reorganized or modified solely for consistency. Changes to published assets require a specific reason and the author's authorization. - This is a technical management document. The author determines which photographs appear in an essay, how many are used, and where they are placed. Technical guidelines do not override literary or editorial decisions. ## 2. Historical authenticity and original photographs - Historical travel photographs are records of the time and place in which they were made. Their original appearance is preserved. Preparation for web publication should ordinarily be limited to resizing, compression, format conversion, and necessary technical corrections. - Visual appeal alone does not justify changes to colors, composition or depicted content. Routine optimization excludes removal, replacement or generation of elements in historical photographs. Any substantive visual edit requires the author's explicit decision. - Original files are preserved in personal storage outside GitHub, with separate optimized web copies and an independent backup. Originals are not overwritten during conversion. - The existing `/assets/images/originals/` directory may contain legacy originals; changes require individual file review. Storage standardization alone does not justify deletion or relocation. - Photographs should serve the author's narrative. There is no fixed number of images per essay. - Meaningful filenames and descriptive `alt` attributes are used. Where appropriate, image dimensions are specified and images below the initial viewport are lazy-loaded. - The author decides photograph selection, count, order, and placement. Rearrangement of photographs, paragraphs or captions requires approval. ## 3. Audio management - Existing recordings and their published references are preserved. - Replacements, renaming or alteration require the author's approval. - New audio is checked for playback, completeness and its relationship to the published work. ## 4. Digital editions - Downloadable PDF and EPUB publication remains suspended under the established policy. - Uploads and updates of downloadable PDF and EPUB editions are suspended until a reliable production and verification process is established. Existing errors in those editions are not to be corrected during this suspension. - The most current literary texts are available in the site's HTML pages. A newly authorized release should be verified for: - Complete text, correct chapter order, and paragraph formatting. - Language-specific fonts, typography, and rendering. - Table of contents and internal navigation. - File opening on representative readers. - Filename, version, and download-link accuracy. - Existing versioned downloads are not silently overwritten. ## 5. File naming - New media use clear, consistent names, but already published files are not renamed merely to achieve uniformity. - Preserving established URLs and originals takes priority over naming uniformity. ## 6. Storage and performance Assets stored in the repository contribute to repository size and, when published, may contribute to GitHub Pages site size and bandwidth. Repository size and published-site size are different measurements. Before publishing a photo essay or substantial new media collection: - Estimation of the total size of all new files. - Confirmation of individual image optimization. - Calculation of the combined size of all images used by the essay, in addition to individual photograph sizes. - Review of likely page-loading impact on mobile connections, including initial images and those loaded later. - Review of relevant repository and GitHub Pages storage constraints. - Avoidance of redundant copies and incremental uploads where useful. - No automatic image-count limit or universal per-essay size cap applies. Total size is a review signal, and the author decides the final composition. Improve compression or loading behavior first if a page is unusually heavy. - Audio and downloadable editions may use more storage than optimized photographs. Storage growth is reviewed periodically. - External hosting is not the default policy. Its adoption requires actual storage, bandwidth or operational needs that justify additional complexity. ## 7. Preservation and change control The existing published website is the reference state. The following practices are excluded: - Moving existing asset directories or renaming published files without approval. - Replacing originals with compressed derivatives. - Automatic recompression, restyling or other editing of published media. - Deletion of apparently unused files without checking all references. - Modification of literary text or paragraph boundaries during asset maintenance. - Changes to photo selection or layout without the author's decision. - Resumption of PDF/EPUB publication without authorization. - Approved changes require prior identification of affected file references, public URLs and cache or indexing consequences, together with confirmation of the latest GitHub `main` and file SHA. Subsequent verification includes retrieval of every changed file and separate confirmation of Pages deployment when feasible. {% endcapture %} {% assign management_html = public_management_snapshot | markdownify %} {% assign management_link_parts = management_html | split: '' | first %}{% assign management_opening_prefix = management_opening_tag | append: '>' %}{% assign management_unlinked = management_link_part | remove_first: management_opening_prefix | remove_first: '' %}{{ management_unlinked }}{% endif %}{% endfor %}{% endcapture %}

Publication Log Policy

{% capture public_management_snapshot %} ## 1. Purpose and authority The Publication Log is a chronological historical record of releases, translations, chapter additions, and significant publication-related changes on Jin-in-Seoul. This document establishes the rules for maintaining that record consistently over time. It governs the Publication Log only; it does not change the names, wording, translation, or publication dates on individual literary pages. The author has final editorial authority. ## 2. Record language and work titles - Log dates, headings, prose and descriptions are written in English. - Each work's established English title serves as its common identifier, including when recording Japanese, French, and Korean editions. - English titles are verified against the live English edition, listing or other approved source before entry. New official titles are not established by guesswork or retranslation. - Where a section heading already identifies the work in English, the language-specific entry may simply say "Japanese edition — Chapters 1–4 added" without repeating its title. - Groups of short stories use the same approved English titles in every language entry. - Appendix titles in the Publication Log use the established official English title, regardless of edition language. The applicable title is Appendix — An Index for Those Lost in the Galaxy for the corresponding Korean and English editions of *A Dictionary of Galactic Extinction*. The edition's own published table of contents may retain its language-specific title. Invented translations and confusion between this common title and individual edition dates or language identities are excluded. - Exceptions: Haiku may retain Japanese originals with English titles; Music may retain the existing English/Korean/Japanese bilingual or multilingual song-title presentation. Standardization of those titles requires author approval. ## 3. Language order When editions or languages share the same date and priority, the following order applies: English → Japanese → French → Korean - This order applies both to language lists within a sentence and to individual edition entries grouped under the same work and date. - When only some languages are present, their relative order follows that sequence. - Separate historical dates retain their order regardless of language priority. - Publication is not inferred from an absent release record. ## 4. Actual publication dates and history - Each edition is recorded on the date it was actually published, not the date of the original work or another translation. - Language editions released on different days retain separate dated entries. - Language releases are not backdated or combined merely to form a four-language entry. - Historical log entries describe what was known, announced, or released at that time. Past plans and publication milestones remain historical records even when plans change; later decisions are documented under their own dates. - Dates, edition languages, chapter/part numbers, release sequence, PDF/EPUB details and the distinction between releases and maintenance events are preserved. - Corrections to factual errors may be made with the author's approval and should not invent a new publication event. ## 5. Event wording The following terms correspond to the event that actually occurred: - added: a newly published work, chapter, language edition, or downloadable edition. - updated: a maintenance or technical change to an existing published file or page without necessarily changing the literary text. - revised: an authorized substantive revision of published literary text. - Neutral release wording is preferred, such as English edition added or French edition added. Publication Log entries exclude complete, completed, full text published, and similar claims of finality; a work or edition may be revised after release. Actual chapter numbers, collection size and publication events are preserved without declaring a work permanently finished. The policy prohibits relabelling an update as a literary revision. ## 6. Titles, subtitles, and consistency - The same approved English title applies wherever a work recurs in the log. - Where a particular log entry includes the work's subtitle, the same approved subtitle applies to later dated entries referring to that release when relevant. A later language release alone does not justify silently dropping a subtitle. - New releases require consistency verification between the English page title and subtitle, index/listing and proposed log entry, rather than conjecture. - Original wording in Haiku and Music is preserved under their exceptions. ## 7. Editing and publishing procedure - Publication dates, approved titles, languages and events are verified against the actual editions. - New entries follow the established chronology and language-order rules. - Review alone does not authorize changes; the author's approval is required. - Historic entries remain intact, and authorized updates are checked for accuracy. ## 8. Scope and safeguards - This policy does not authorize edits to the underlying literary works, translations, original photographs, audio, PDF/EPUB files, site layouts, or unrelated navigation. - Historical formatting cleanup must not change a release's meaning or its actual date. - Uncertain English translations of formerly non-English-only titles require verification or author approval, not invented equivalents. - Apparent inconsistencies require review of actual source pages and publication history before either entry is changed. - The author may revise this policy; changes should be made explicitly and documented in the repository. {% endcapture %} {% assign management_html = public_management_snapshot | markdownify %} {% assign management_link_parts = management_html | split: '' | first %}{% assign management_opening_prefix = management_opening_tag | append: '>' %}{% assign management_unlinked = management_link_part | remove_first: management_opening_prefix | remove_first: '' %}{{ management_unlinked }}{% endif %}{% endfor %}{% endcapture %}

Translation and Editing Principles for Premodern Korean Fiction

{% capture public_management_snapshot %} In this document, premodern Korean fiction refers to Korean fiction written before the modern era. The term denotes a historical period rather than a judgment of literary merit or canonical status. ### 1. Source Text A source edition is established for each work, with its title, date of publication, and source identified. Material from different editions is not arbitrarily combined. ### 2. Original Names and Titles The titles of works and the names of characters follow the source text rather than their more widely known forms. This principle applies to both modern Korean and foreign-language translations. ### 3. Preservation of Content Events, characters, dialogue, actions, descriptions, and figurative expressions are not arbitrarily omitted or abridged. Nothing absent from the source text is added. ### 4. Historical Values Characters’ actions and events are not embellished or softened to conform to contemporary ethical, political, or social values. The values, human relationships, social structures, and religious worldviews of the source text are preserved. ### 5. Modern Korean Vocabulary and sentence structures accessible to modern readers are used while preserving the style, atmosphere, and voices of the original. Accurate transmission of meaning takes precedence over the retention of archaic linguistic forms. ### 6. Reading Without Annotations Difficult archaic expressions, official titles, and religious terms are rendered in accessible language so that the text can be read without separate annotations or footnotes. Terms intelligible from context are retained, with their meanings conveyed naturally upon first appearance where necessary. Historical official titles and distinctive names must not disappear through paraphrase; the name is retained and its meaning conveyed naturally at first appearance when needed. ### 7. Chapters and Paragraphs The text may be divided into chapters and given chapter titles according to the development of events and the narrative flow. Paragraphs are arranged according to the continuity of dialogue, actions, and descriptions. The sequence of events and the narrative structure remain unchanged. Once the modern Korean edition is finalized, foreign-language editions preserve its paragraph boundaries one-to-one, without splitting or merging paragraphs to follow target-language conventions. ### 8. Dialogue Attribution and Repetition Repetitive expressions such as “said,” “asked,” and “replied” may be reduced when the speaker is clear. Expressions necessary to identify speakers or convey their emotions and actions are retained. Repetition that serves a literary purpose is preserved. ### 9. No Adaptation Works are not adapted for specific readerships, including children, young adults, or students. The content of the source text is not altered for moral, educational, or commercial purposes. ### 10. Foreign-Language Translation Every translation into another language is made directly from the finalized modern Korean edition. Events, characters, dialogue, descriptions, figurative expressions, paragraph structure, and narrative rhythm are preserved. Korean historical and cultural elements are not replaced with those of the target culture, and the rendering of personal names, place names, official titles, and objects remains consistent throughout each work. If an error in the modern Korean edition is discovered during translation, the source text is consulted, the Korean edition is corrected first, and the correction is incorporated into all foreign-language editions. ### 11. Inconsistencies and Ambiguities Inconsistencies in ages, chronology, or events within the source text are not arbitrarily corrected. Ambiguous expressions are not clarified through conjecture. Inconsistencies inherent in the source text are distinguished from errors introduced during translation. ### 12. Interpretation and Verification Difficult archaic expressions and damaged passages are examined using dictionaries, relevant documents, and their surrounding context. Other editions and existing translations may be consulted as references for interpretation. Unverified content is not supplied through conjecture. ### 13. Review and Revision Revision is undertaken to correct mistranslations, omissions, distortions of meaning, and defects in expression. Natural sentences that faithfully convey the original meaning are not repeatedly revised according to the personal preferences of translators or editors. The same editorial principles apply to the modern Korean and foreign-language editions. Proposed changes are reviewed and approved before application, and the revised text is compared again with the established source. --- # Shared Practical Translation Standards and English Glossary ## I. Shared Translation Standards ### 1. Source text Translate directly from the established modern Korean edition. Consult the source edition for interpretation or error verification. If the Korean edition needs correction, present the proposed correction for review and obtain approval before applying it. Do not repeatedly revise established translations according to personal preference. ### 2. Style Use modern English while retaining the character of a tale from premodern Korea. Avoid artificial archaism, contemporary American colloquialism and Western fantasy adaptation. Establish the narrator's voice and sentence rhythm separately for each work. ### 3. Events and magical arts The stranger the event, the more matter-of-fact the narration. Do not add light, sound, movement, emotion, suspense or explanations absent from the source. Preserve the order in which events unfold and information reaches the reader. ### 4. Dialogue and hierarchy Reflect relations between sovereign and subject, parent and child, teacher and disciple, and men and women in tone and sentence structure. Do not translate every form of address mechanically. Do not add dialogue tags where the speaker is clear; preserve meaningful repetition. ### 5. Names and concepts Use personal names, place names and names of identical institutions and objects consistently. Distinguish differing meanings or ranks according to context even when the same term is used. Reveal the identity of supernatural beings only to the extent known to the narrator or speaker. ### 6. Imagery and historical values Preserve East Asian imagery, exaggeration, religious worldviews and values such as filial devotion, loyalty and chastity. Do not add colours, textures or psychological details not specified in the source. Do not soften the text to conform to modern ethics or substitute Western cultural elements. ### 7. Units and form Do not arbitrarily convert traditional units into Western units. Preserve the established Korean edition's numbering, titles, paragraphs, event sequence, deliberate ambiguities and source inconsistencies. Use context to make the work readable without separate annotations. ### 8. English spelling and expression Use British spelling as the default. Conventional romanisations of Korean surnames are permitted and remain consistent within a work. Do not add intensifiers or modifiers absent from the source. Convey forms of address such as 生 and 兄 according to rank and relationships. Capitalise formal names of government offices. ### 9. Magical arts and traditional concepts Distinguish changes in appearance, separation of bodies, and movement between paintings and reality. Preserve the cultural significance of traditional colours, including the Five Phases, according to context. Do not adapt an ending to strengthen its literary resonance. ### 10. Final comparison Compare individual paragraph contents and boundaries, not just paragraph counts, with the established Korean edition. Apply corrections after review and approval and verify them again. ## II. Shared English Glossary The following tables are the current proposed translation standards.
