Thought Experiment Notes

A Record of 26 Questions and Answers · English · 한국어

The scientific premises of A Dictionary of Galactic Extinction began with a question: What would happen if a single candle were lit inside a sealed room lined with mirrors? A candle emits a certain amount of light. But if that light were not absorbed by the walls and instead continued to reflect, how would the amount of light present in the room change? Examining this question led me to consider the conservation of light, the accumulation of energy, gravity, black holes, separate spacetimes, the direction of time, and the future of the universe.

The original ideas underwent several revisions as the examination of their physical implications progressed. The idea of storing energy inside a small sphere changed into a proposal to use the sphere as an entrance to another spacetime. The attempt to explain the energy consumption of galactic civilization and the photon deficit as a single phenomenon was also revised. After examining a scenario in which a scientist from the distant future travels back in time to take energy from the galaxy, the story developed in a direction that made use of the existing galactic transportation network.

These notes document the process of those changes. They bring together concepts from physics consulted during the investigation, theories that have yet to be confirmed, and hypotheses adopted for the novel. They record an attempt to establish how far scientific explanation can go, and what additional assumptions are needed for the story to work beyond those limits.
