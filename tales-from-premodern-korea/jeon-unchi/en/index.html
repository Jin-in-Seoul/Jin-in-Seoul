Tales from Premodern Korea

The Tale of Jeon Unchi

English · 한국어

Generally known as Jeon Uchijeon, this premodern Korean novel is of unknown authorship and date of composition, and survives in several editions. The 37-leaf, 22-leaf and 17-leaf Gyeongpan editions, as well as the manuscript held in the Ilsa Collection at Seoul National University, bear the title 뎐운치젼, while the movable-type edition published by Sinmungwan in 1914 bears the title 뎐우치젼. The protagonist’s name, the structure and the content of the story vary between editions. Books published in Korea today generally use the title Jeon Uchijeon. Some also call the protagonist Jeon Uchi in the text, while others retain the name Jeon Unchi despite using the title Jeon Uchijeon.

The edition of The Tale of Jeon Unchi published by Jin-in-Seoul is based on the 37-leaf Gyeongpan edition of 뎐운치젼, printed in 1847. Both the title of the work and the protagonist’s name follow the source edition. The chapter divisions and titles have been newly established.

1. The Fox’s Bead

Towards the end of the Goryeo dynasty, there lived a renowned scholar in Namseobu. His surname was Jeon, his given name was Suk, and he was known as Master Unhwa. He came from a family that had held high office for generations, but when it came to Suk, he had no desire to enter government service. He withdrew into the mountains and devoted himself to learning. From time to time, he gathered his friends to speak of mountains and rivers, wind and moon, and so passed his days. The people of the time called him a mountain recluse. His wife, Lady Choi, was the daughter of a distinguished family, gentle and virtuous, possessing both beauty and virtue. The scholar and his wife respected each other and lived in harmony, but although they had been together for more than ten years, they had no children and lamented this day and night.

One day, Lady Choi had a dream. A bank of clouds descended from Heaven, and from among them emerged a boy dressed in blue, holding a blue lotus. He bowed twice before her and said, “I am a young immortal who gathered medicinal herbs on Mount Yeongju. Having committed an offence in Heaven, I have been banished to the human world. I have nowhere to go. I beg you to take pity on me.”

Lady Choi was overjoyed and was about to question him further when she suddenly awoke. Still in a state of wonder, she called her husband and told him of her dream. Upon hearing it, the scholar said, “Our unfortunate fate has made us sorrowful for fear that we might have no descendants. Now that you have had such a dream, Heaven must surely have granted us a precious child.” He was exceedingly pleased.

Indeed, she conceived that very month, and ten months passed. One day, clouds of five colours surrounded the house, and a fragrance filled the air. The scholar swept the main house clean and waited for the appointed time. In her dazed state, Lady Choi raised her eyes and saw the boy from her dream entering the room. Overcome with joy, she lost consciousness and presently gave birth to a beautiful baby boy, a full ja in length. The scholar was delighted, attending to his wife while examining the child. Seeing his fine features and sturdy frame, he said joyfully, “This is the boy who appeared in your dream. We shall name him Unchi, give him the courtesy name Mongjungseon and the literary name Gusipjae.”

Their love and affection for the child were beyond compare. When Unchi reached the age of seven, his father began instructing him in letters. The boy was intelligent and quick-witted: hearing one thing, he understood ten. His father loved him dearly.

Unchi reached the age of ten. Alas! When joy comes to an end, sorrow follows, as it has throughout the ages. The scholar suddenly fell ill. When none of the medicines he took proved effective, he called his wife and said, “I fear I shall soon belong to the world of the dead. My greatest sorrow is that I shall not see our child grow to manhood. You must restrain your grief and not disregard my last wishes. Raise Unchi well, live to see him attain honour, uphold the ancestral rites, and spend your days in peace until the age of a hundred.” Hearing these words, his wife wept aloud and could not speak.

A few days later, the scholar passed away. His wife beat her breast and wailed, while Unchi cried out to Heaven in grief and lost consciousness several times. Though her own sorrow was beyond measure, Lady Choi worried about her young son and did everything she could to comfort him. Despite his tender age, Unchi neglected none of the prescribed funeral rites. He conducted the funeral with the utmost care, buried his father in the ancestral burial grounds, and attended to his mother with such filial devotion throughout the three years of mourning that the villagers were filled with admiration.

Among the late scholar Jeon’s close friends was a man named Master Yun. He excelled in letters and learning and possessed the insight to see ten thousand ri ahead. Unchi took his books to Master Yun and studied under him.

One day, Unchi rose early and set out for his school with his books. As he crossed a mountain, he came upon a woman sitting and weeping among thick bamboo, neatly dressed in white mourning clothes. Pretending not to see her, Unchi continued on his way and studied with Master Yun. When he had finished his lessons and was returning home, he saw that the woman was still weeping. Finding this strange, he approached her. She appeared to be fifteen or sixteen years old. Her features were like jade, and her graceful figure was enough to stir a man’s heart. Unchi drew near and sought to comfort her, asking, “Where do you live, and what has caused you to weep so sorrowfully from morning until midday?”

The woman stopped weeping and replied with an expression of embarrassment. “I live at the foot of this mountain. I have suffered a sorrowful misfortune, and that is why I weep.”

When she would say no more, Unchi moved closer and questioned her earnestly. At last, she answered reluctantly, “I am the daughter of Inspector Maeng. I lost my mother when I was five. Ever since my stepmother came into our household, she has spoken ill of me to my father and sought to have me killed. I have been miserable day and night and have thought of taking my own life, but I could not bring myself to do so. That is why you find me weeping here.”

Taking great pity on her, Unchi said, “Life and death are matters of fate. Think of the body your parents gave you and seek a way to live.”

Unchi took her jade-like hand, and the woman showed no sign of coldness towards him. The two willingly lay together and took pleasure in each other. When the time came to part, they went their separate ways with great reluctance.

The following day, as Unchi was on his way to Master Yun, the woman appeared at the same place and called to him. “I have been here waiting for you for a long time.”

Delighted to see her, Unchi took her hand and spent some pleasant moments with her before saying, “Wait here for a little while.”

When Unchi arrived at the school, Master Yun said, “You have indulged in carnal pleasures on your way here. Even if you pursue your studies, you will not comprehend the workings of Heaven and Earth. When you return, you will meet that woman again. There will be a bead in her mouth. Take it from her and bring it to me.”

Following his teacher’s instructions, Unchi met the woman on his way home. He took her jade-like hand and entered the bamboo grove with her. While they exchanged their affections, he watched her closely and saw that there was indeed a bead in her mouth. He asked to see it, but she would not show it to him. Unchi grew serious and said, “You are an unmarried maiden, and I too am unmarried. I wish us to speak to our parents and grow old together like a pair of mandarin ducks. Why will you not grant my wish?”

Hearing these words, the woman could not resist her affection for him. She kissed Unchi, extended her tongue, and rolled the bead into his mouth. When Unchi kept the bead in his mouth for a long time without returning it, she urged him to give it back. Still he would not return it, so she tried to open his mouth and retrieve the bead.

At that moment, Unchi swallowed it. Seeing that the bead was gone, the woman could not utter a word. She burst into loud tears and went down towards the fields.

Unchi was uneasy at heart. He returned to Master Yun and told him everything that had happened from beginning to end. Master Yun said, “Now that you have swallowed the fox’s vital essence, you will understand astronomy and geography and be able to practise seventy-two arts of transformation. You will also pass the examination for the jinsa degree in the fourth month of this year. From now on, be cautious in all your affairs.”

By the age of fifteen, Unchi’s literary compositions surpassed those of Li Bai, and his calligraphy rivalled that of Wang Xizhi. Having swallowed the fox’s vital essence, he had also mastered thirty-six magical arts. At this time, the kingdom held a civil service examination. Unchi entered the examination hall, submitted his composition, and passed in first place.

After three days of celebrations for the successful candidates, he returned home to see his mother. Lady Choi was both joyful and sorrowful and said, “Your father never cared for the civil service examinations when he was alive. Now that I see you attain such honour, how could I not be pleased?”

2. The Nine-Tailed Fox and the Heavenly Books

Time passed, and the following spring arrived. Unchi travelled among famous mountains and great rivers until he came to a temple called Segeumsa. Its halls, extending over more than a thousand bays, were covered in cobwebs, and not a single monk was to be seen. Finding this strange, Unchi went down to Seongnimsa Temple. Four or five elderly monks came out to greet him. When he asked about Segeumsa, one of the monks said, “There were once more than a thousand monks between Segeumsa and this temple, but calamities began to befall both temples four or five years ago. Unable to endure them, some of the monks scattered, while others disappeared without a trace. Segeumsa is now entirely deserted, and only the four or five of us old monks remain here.”

“Some evil creature must surely be causing mischief.”

When he returned home and told his mother about Segeumsa, she said, “Be careful from now on.”

Thereafter, Unchi devoted himself to farming and supporting his mother. One day, he told her that he wished to study at Segeumsa and sit the civil service examination the following year. His mother said, “I have heard that there are many evil creatures in that temple that harm people. Why would you wish to go there?”

“Evil cannot harm what is righteous. How could some insignificant creature harm me? Mother, please do not worry too much.”

He packed his belongings at once and set out for Segeumsa. At one point along the way, he saw an old man standing at ease upon a high, steep cliff. The man wore a hood woven from kudzu fibres and simple clothes, and leaned upon a staff made from the stalk of a goosefoot plant. Unchi approached and paid his respects. The old man asked, “Who are you, that you should take the trouble to pay your respects to me?”

“How could I pass by without a greeting when an elder such as yourself is standing here?”

The old man said, “I have something to give you and have been waiting here for you for a long time.” He took from his sleeve a cord called Buyongseung and a talisman, handed them to Unchi, and said, “An occasion to use them will present itself.” As soon as he had spoken, he vanished without a trace.

Unchi offered his thanks to the empty air and entered Segeumsa with the cord and the talisman. He had his attendant clean a room and asked a monk from Seongnimsa to bring him his evening meal. After eating, he lit a candle and began to read.

At midnight, the door suddenly opened, and a woman entered and calmly seated herself beside him. Unchi raised his eyes and saw that she appeared to be about fourteen years old. Her splendid beauty was like that of a peony bearing the morning dew, and her slender figure resembled a weeping willow unable to withstand even the spring breeze. She was beautiful enough to melt a man’s heart. Entranced, Unchi asked, “Where do you live, and what brings you here at this late hour?”

The woman replied, “I come from a family of scholar-officials. I was accompanying my husband to Jangyang, where he was to take up his appointment as magistrate, when we encountered bandits. My entire family was killed, and all our belongings were lost. I alone escaped with my life. I have been hiding in the mountains by day and travelling by night, trying to find my way home. From a distance, I saw the candlelight shining through the window and came here, thinking this was someone’s house. I could clearly hear a man reading, but I was so exhausted that I entered without regard for propriety. If you will save my life, I shall repay your kindness even after death.”

“Misfortune and good fortune are beyond our control. It is fortunate that you have escaped such a calamity and reached this place. Where is your home, and how old are you?”

The woman said, “My home is outside the southern gate of the capital, and I am seventeen.”

“Then we are of the same age. The capital is more than three hundred ri from here. How will you, a woman travelling alone, ever reach it? It is indeed a matter of concern.”

The woman sighed and said, “I beg you to take pity on my circumstances and allow me to stay for just one night.”

“My family is poor, and I have not yet married. I had thought that, should I be fortunate enough to pass the examination next spring, I might then marry. It must be fate that has brought us together tonight. What would you say to becoming husband and wife and spending a hundred years together?”

The woman lowered her head and said nothing. Her bashful appearance was all the more beautiful in the candlelight. Unchi pushed his reading table aside and said, “I spoke without forethought, and my words have offended you so greatly that I find myself embarrassed instead. Consider the matter carefully and do not spoil your future.”

After thinking for a long while, the woman said, “Though my circumstances are desperate, I too am descended from a family of scholars. I would rather die than willingly submit to dishonour. I am deeply grateful for your words. If you will one day avenge my family, how could I refuse your wishes?”

Hearing this, Unchi was overcome with excitement and lay with the woman.

“Today is an auspicious day. Let us share a cup of wine to seal our marriage and swear an oath before Heaven and Earth.” He filled a cup from a bamboo wine flask and drank it himself, then poured another and offered it to her. The woman dared not refuse and drank it. When Unchi offered her another cup, she firmly declined.

Unchi said, “What harm can there be in drinking a cup or two of wine?”

The woman drank reluctantly. Unchi drank another cup and offered her yet another, but she refused, saying that she would rather die than drink it. Unchi grew serious and said, “When a woman has agreed to follow her husband, it is only proper that she obey him. Why are you behaving so discourteously?”

Seeing the expression on his face, the woman reluctantly accepted the cup and drank. Presently, she lost consciousness, collapsed, and began to snore. Only then did Unchi remove her clothes and write an incantation upon her chest with a red writing brush. When he saw that it left no trace, he became certain that she was a fox. He took out the Buyongseung cord, bound her hands and feet, pricked her with an awl, and struck her with a club. The woman awoke in alarm and cried out, “My lord! What is the meaning of this?”

Unchi rebuked her fiercely. “You wicked she-fox! You have brought calamity upon this temple and harmed living creatures. I have long been waiting here to kill you and rid the human world of the harm you cause.”

Unchi pricked her all over with the awl until the creature could endure it no longer and revealed her true form. Golden fur appeared upon her body, and she became a fox with nine tails and begged for her life. Unchi said, “Give me one of your vital essences, and I shall spare your life.”

“My vital essence is within my belly, but I possess something even better: three heavenly books. I beg you to spare my life.”

Unchi was, by nature, a scholar. Delighted at the mention of books, he asked, “Where are these books?”

“They are in my cave. If you release me, I shall fetch them.”

Unchi grew furious and pricked her again with the awl, whereupon the creature said, “If you untie only my feet, I shall go there with you and give you the books.”

Finding her proposal reasonable, Unchi untied her feet and followed the fox. On a great mountain stood an enormous rock, beneath which there was a cave. They went about five ri into the cave and came to a place where green pines and bamboo grew thickly and a stream flowed gently. There were countless houses, each adorned with brilliantly coloured painted patterns. As Unchi entered with the fox walking ahead of him, a maidservant dressed in colourful clothes came out to greet her. “My lady, you went hunting today and have brought back a man. We shall enjoy eating him.”

When the maidservant rushed towards him, Unchi grew furious and struck the lesser creatures dead one by one. He again pricked the nine-tailed fox with the awl. Unable to endure it, she said to the maidservant, “Go quickly and fetch the three books from the treasure chest.”

The creature hurried away and brought the books. Unchi examined them and saw that they were indeed heavenly books. Unable to decipher the characters, he asked the nine-tailed fox to teach him their meaning. “If you untie my hands, I shall teach you.”

Unchi pricked her with the awl and raised his club, whereupon the nine-tailed fox agreed to teach him. Without untying the cord, Unchi said, “Let us go to the temple where I am staying.”

He took the nine-tailed fox back to Segeumsa. After drinking some wine, he seated her before him and studied the first volume of the heavenly books. In the course of a single night, he mastered it entirely. These were truly magical arts that even spirits could not fathom. Only then did Unchi untie the cord binding the fox. He removed the talisman affixed to her back, placed it upon the first volume of the heavenly books, and said, “I intended to kill you to prevent further trouble, but instead I have received a great kindness from you, and so I shall spare your life and let you go. Do not bring calamity upon anyone again.” The nine-tailed fox thanked him and departed.

Presently, a great wind arose, the door opened, and a voice was heard from within a blue cloud. “Gusipjae! I shall take back my Buyongseung and leave you the talisman.”

Unchi hurried outside and saw the blue cloud rising into the sky. He offered his thanks to the empty air and returned to his room. Suddenly, a scholar came riding in on a donkey and dismounted before the stone steps. It was Master Yun. Unchi hurried to greet him, and as they spoke, Master Yun asked, “This is a book that no scholar ought to read. Why are you reading it?” Before Unchi could answer, Master Yun vanished without a trace. Greatly astonished, Unchi examined the heavenly books and found that one of them was missing.

While he was still wondering about this, he suddenly heard a woman weeping nearby. When he went outside, he saw his old nurse with her hair hanging loose. She was crying as she spoke. “Your mother was perfectly well until yesterday, but she passed away during the night. Young master, we must leave at once.”

Greatly alarmed, Unchi hurriedly gathered his books. In the blink of an eye, however, his nurse disappeared, and another of the heavenly books was gone. Unchi grew furious and said, “That wicked creature has made a fool of me and deceived me in this manner. I shall go to the fox’s cave, recover the books, and destroy her.”

Taking his club and awl, he went in search of the fox’s cave. The mountains and valleys were deep, and the paths stretched far into the distance. He could not find the cave anywhere. Returning to the temple, he thought to himself, ‘There is no fathoming this creature’s arts of transformation. I must not remain here for long.’

Unchi gathered his books and returned home. The talisman was affixed to the first volume of the heavenly books, which was why the nine-tailed fox had been unable to take it.

3. The Golden Beam

Unchi returned home and studied the heavenly books until there was no magical art he could not perform. Having lost interest in studying for the civil service examinations, he thought to himself, ‘If I am to obtain a government post and support my mother, it will take a rather long time.’ He then devised a plan.

Unchi shook his body and transformed himself into an immortal. Riding clouds of five colours, he rose into the air and entered the royal palace. When he reached the centre of Daemyeong Hall, an auspicious presence filled the air. The people in the palace were astonished and did not know what to do. The officials reported to the king, “This is a remarkable occurrence, rarely seen in ancient or modern times.”

Greatly astonished, the king gathered his officials to discuss the matter. Unchi was standing among clouds and mist when a boy dressed in blue cried out, “King of Goryeo, hear the command of the Jade Emperor!” The king had a place prepared and a table set with an incense burner before coming outside. An immortal wearing a golden crown and a red robe stood solemnly amid clouds of five colours, with attendant boys on either side.

The king bowed four times and prostrated himself. Unchi said, “A magnificent pavilion beside the pond of Yaochi in the heavenly realm has grown old and fallen into ruin over the ages. It is now to be repaired. Commands have been issued to the various kingdoms of the human world, and each has presented the required materials. All that remains is a single golden beam. Knowing that your kingdom abounds in gold, the Jade Emperor has now issued his command. The beam is to be raised at around midday on the seventh day of the seventh month. Have it ready by that day. It must be ten ja and five chi in length and three ja and two chi in width. Should you fail to meet the appointed date, he will bring a great calamity upon you.”

As soon as he had spoken, the gentle sound of heavenly music filled the air, and the clouds of five colours drifted southwards and disappeared. The king bowed four times towards the southern sky, returned to the royal hall, and gathered his civil and military officials to discuss the matter. One official said, “It would seem proper to issue orders to the eight provinces to collect gold and fulfil the command of Heaven.”

The king agreed and immediately issued orders to the eight provinces. Gold was collected and craftsmen summoned to make a beam of the prescribed length and width and present it by the appointed day. The king purified his body and mind for three days and awaited its arrival.

Between seven and nine in the morning on the appointed day, clouds of five colours filled the palace grounds, and a fragrance permeated the air. The immortal appeared majestically amid the clouds. The boys dressed in blue on either side descended upon cranes, attached hooks to the golden beam, and raised it into the air. As the beam, surrounded by colourful clouds, moved towards the southern sky, a long rainbow stretched across the heavens, and the clouds of five colours dispersed eastwards and westwards. The king and his officials approached the incense table and bowed four times. The king then returned to the royal hall and received the congratulations of his officials.

Unchi had deceived the king and obtained the golden beam, but the kingdom’s supply of gold had already been exhausted, and he would arouse great suspicion if he tried to sell it. He suddenly devised a plan, cut off one end of the beam, and entered the city to sell it. A guard of the Police Bureau happened to see him and asked suspiciously, “Where did this gold come from, and how much do you want for it?”

“The place from which the gold came is elsewhere. The price is five hundred geum.”

“If you tell me where you live, I shall come tomorrow with the money.”

“My house is in Namseobu of Mount Songak, and my name is Jeon Unchi.”

Having made the arrangement, the police officer returned to the government office and reported the matter. The magistrate said, “There must be something behind this. I shall investigate carefully and then apprehend the fellow.” He first gave the police officer five hundred nyang of silver and ordered him to purchase the gold. The officer immediately went to Namseobu, met Unchi, and handed him the silver. Unchi gave him the gold and received the silver.

When the police officer returned and reported to the magistrate, the magistrate examined the gold and exclaimed in astonishment, “This is undoubtedly one end of the golden beam. I shall have him arrested, ascertain the truth, and report it to the royal court.”

The magistrate dispatched some ten military officers and officers of the Police Bureau. When they arrived in Namseobu to arrest Unchi, he entertained them generously with food and said, “You have taken the trouble to come all this way, but I shall not go with you. No matter how hard your magistrate tries, he will not be able to apprehend me. When an order comes from the king, I shall submit to arrest.” Unchi remained entirely unperturbed.

The officers dared not lay a hand upon him. They returned and reported the matter to the magistrate, who was greatly astonished. He mobilised five hundred local soldiers and ordered them to surround Unchi’s house and apprehend him, while also reporting the matter to the royal court. The king was furious. He gathered his officials to discuss the matter and ordered the Royal Tribunal to arrest Unchi.

Meanwhile, having obtained the silver, Unchi was preparing food for his mother. He suddenly learned that an order had come from the capital for his arrest. While he was devising a plan, an officer of the Royal Tribunal and officers of the Police Bureau arrived with local soldiers, watching his movements and preparing to apprehend him. Unchi brought out a small, slender bottle used for holding ink and said to his mother, “Please enter this bottle at once.”

His mother entered the bottle, and Unchi followed her inside. Finding this exceedingly strange, the officers rushed forward and tightly sealed its mouth. Taking the bottle with them, they travelled day and night towards the capital. A voice came from within the bottle. “I entered this bottle to escape trouble. Who has sealed its mouth? I shall suffocate. Remove the stopper at once!”

The officer of the Royal Tribunal pretended not to hear and hurried onwards until he reached the palace. He appeared before the king and recounted the circumstances of Unchi’s arrest from beginning to end. The king said, “Though Unchi possesses magical powers, how can a man possibly enter a bottle?”

Unchi then cried out from within the bottle, “I can scarcely breathe. Please remove the stopper!”

Only then did the king realise that Unchi was indeed inside the bottle. He asked his officials how the matter should be dealt with. They replied, “His powers of sorcery are beyond comprehension. We fear that if he is handled carelessly, he may escape.”

The king ordered oil to be brought to a boil in a great cauldron and the bottle placed inside it. Unchi cried out from within the bottle, “My household is poor, and I have lived shivering with cold day and night. Today I have been brought into a warm place where I can warm myself. The kingdom’s grace knows no bounds!”

They boiled the bottle from morning until evening, until all the oil had boiled away. The king ordered the bottle to be broken. It shattered into several pieces, but there was nothing inside. The fragments then ran individually towards the king and cried out, “Your servant Jeon Unchi is here!”

The king flew into a rage and commanded, “Grind all those fragments into powder and boil them in oil!” He also ordered that Jeon Unchi’s house be demolished, that its site be dug out to make a pond, and that Unchi be apprehended.

A high official then addressed the king. “This sorcerer cannot be apprehended. If Your Majesty wishes to prevent further trouble, it would be advisable to post proclamations upon the four gates of the city, declaring that if Unchi presents himself voluntarily, his crimes will be pardoned and he will be granted a government post. Should he appear, entrust him with an important duty. If he commits another offence, it would then be proper to put him to death.”

The king agreed and immediately ordered proclamations to be posted upon the four gates of the city. They read: ‘Although Jeon Unchi has committed offences against the kingdom, in recognition of his talents he is hereby granted a special pardon and appointed to government office. Let him present himself without delay.’

4. The Wandering Magician

Unchi took his mother into the mountains, where they lived upon the silver. From time to time, he rode upon clouds and travelled freely in all directions.

One day, he came upon a white-haired old man weeping bitterly. Unchi approached and asked the reason for his sorrow. The old man said, “I am seventy years old and have only one child. My son has been falsely accused of murder and imprisoned. That is why I grieve.”

When Unchi asked him to explain the circumstances of the injustice in detail, the old man said, “In our village there is a man named Wang whose wife was beautiful. My son became intimate with her and visited her regularly. The woman was promiscuous and also became intimate with a man named Jo, but her husband discovered them. The two men were fighting and striking each other when my son happened to arrive. He separated them and sent Jo away. Wang then died on the spot, and his cousin reported the matter to the authorities as a murder. Jo is a retainer of Yang Mungi and has connections, so he escaped punishment. Documents were drawn up accusing my son of killing Wang, and he was imprisoned. That is why I am so distressed.”

“If what you say is true, I shall see that your son comes to no harm.”

After parting from the old man, Unchi shook his body, transformed himself into a gentle breeze, and went to Yang Mungi’s house. Yang Mungi happened to be in the outer quarters, examining his face in a mirror. Unchi transformed himself into the likeness of the deceased Wang and stood beside him. Finding this strange, Yang Mungi put down the mirror and looked around, but there was nothing there. ‘How strange that some creature should make sport of me in broad daylight.’ When he looked into the mirror again, the man he had seen before was standing there. “I am Wang, who recently died at the hands of Jo. You have judged the matter wrongly, imprisoned the innocent Yi, and released Jo. Unless you avenge my death upon Jo, I shall not remain idle.” As soon as he had spoken, he vanished. Yang Mungi was greatly alarmed. He immediately opened the government office, had Jo arrested, and subjected him to severe questioning.

Just as Jo was protesting his innocence and attempting to evade responsibility, Wang appeared again and cried out, “You wretched Jo! Why did you violate my wife and then kill me? This is the cause of my deep resentment. How dare you lay the blame upon the innocent Yi?” As soon as he had spoken, he disappeared. Jo was overcome with bewilderment, and Yang Mungi was greatly astonished. When Yang Mungi questioned Jo under severe punishment, Jo could endure it no longer and confessed everything in detail. Yi was immediately released, and Jo was executed.

Having rescued Yi, Unchi again rode upon a cloud and looked down below. In a marketplace, people were quarrelling over pigs’ heads. Unchi descended and asked what the dispute was about. One man replied, “I have need of a pig’s head and was the first to agree upon a price, but that man is relying upon his authority as a government official to take it from me. That is why we are quarrelling.”

Wishing to play a trick upon the official, Unchi recited an incantation. All the pigs’ heads opened their mouths and rushed towards the official to bite him. Greatly alarmed, he fled.

Unchi came to another place where music resounded loudly and singing continued without interruption. He approached, paid his respects, and said, “I am a traveller passing through. I would like to watch your festivities.”

The scholars returned his greeting, and they introduced themselves to one another. Unchi raised his eyes and saw some ten gisaeng, each playing a musical instrument and singing. Among those present were two scholars, So and Seol, who behaved with great arrogance and conceit. Unchi laughed at them inwardly and conversed with the other scholars. Presently, wine and food were brought out, and Unchi said, “Thanks to your hospitality, I have been able to taste these rare delicacies. I am grateful.”

Seol said, “Though we are poor, we have many celebrated gisaeng and rare delicacies. I imagine you have never witnessed such a spectacle before.”

Unchi smiled and said, “That may be so, yet there are still many things lacking.”

Seol asked, “What is lacking?”

“For a start, there are no cool watermelons, tart peaches, or sweet grapes. How can you say that everything has been properly provided?”

The scholars laughed loudly and said, “You are indeed thoughtless. It is late spring. Where could such fruits possibly be found?”

Unchi said, “I have seen a place where all manner of fruits are growing.”

Seol asked, “Then can you go and buy some now?”

“If I bring them back, let us make a substantial wager.”

Unchi took a boy attendant with him and went to a garden. There were peaches growing upon the trees. He had the boy climb a tree, pick the peaches, and carry them on his back. Below the trees, grapes hung from a trellis. He had the boy pick those and carry them as well. When they went down into the fields, watermelons were growing upon their vines. Unchi also had twenty watermelons picked and loaded onto the boy’s back before returning. The people were greatly astonished. As they ate the fruits, they marvelled at what had happened.

When Unchi had become quite drunk, he decided to play a trick upon So and Seol and recited an incantation towards them. Presently, the two men said, “Our bodies feel exceedingly heavy, and our minds are in confusion. How strange!”

“You two behave arrogantly, but it seems you no longer have any need of gisaeng.”

The two men grew angry and said, “We are not eunuchs. Why do you say we have no need of gisaeng?”

Unchi laughed and said, “Do not be angry. Put your hands inside your trousers and feel for yourselves.”

Seol did as he was told and said to So, “My manhood has vanished without a trace, and everything is quite flat. What is the meaning of this?”

So said, “Let me see.”

Seol showed him, and indeed there was nothing there. So felt his own manhood and discovered that the same thing had happened to him. Greatly alarmed, the two men said, “Brother Jeon was mocking us earlier, and now this calamity has indeed befallen us. What are we to do?”

One of the gisaeng also found that her genitals had disappeared and that a hole had appeared upon her belly. She did not know what to do. Among those present was a scholar named Eun, who was exceptionally intelligent and learned. Suddenly realising what had happened, he begged Unchi for forgiveness. “We have been blind and have offended you. We beg you to forgive us.”

“Do not worry. Everything will return to its former state of its own accord.”

The two scholars and the gisaeng were delighted. When they examined their bodies, they found them as they had been before. Everyone thanked Unchi, saying, “We did not realise that an immortal had descended among us, and we nearly ended up with damaged bodies.”

Unchi again rode upon a cloud and headed eastwards. As he passed one place, he heard two or three people gathered together in discussion. “Jang, the keeper of the treasury, is a good man of great filial devotion. It would be a grievous and terrible thing if he were unjustly put to death.”

Unchi descended and asked what had happened. One man replied, “Jang Gyechang, a treasury keeper of the Ministry of Revenue, is a benevolent man of filial devotion who takes pleasure in helping those in difficulty. Owing to an error in the preparation of some documents, he has been made liable for two thousand nyang of silver that he never spent. They say he is to be executed for the offence, and that is why we are distressed.”

Taking pity on him, Unchi again rode upon a cloud and went to the execution ground to wait. Sure enough, a young man was brought upon a cart, followed by a young woman who was weeping. Unchi questioned the people and learned that the young man was indeed Jang Gyechang. As Unchi watched, the guards brought the prisoner down from the cart and announced that the hour of execution had arrived. Unchi transformed himself into a gust of wind, swept up Jang Gyechang and his wife, and rose into the sky. The official overseeing the execution was greatly alarmed and reported exactly what had happened to the king. The king was astonished, and the officials of the court were bewildered. Unchi returned home, set the couple down, and gave them medicine dissolved in water. Presently, they regained consciousness, though neither knew what had happened. Unchi recounted everything from beginning to end and also explained the circumstances to his mother.

Unchi was again travelling upon a cloud when he saw a man weeping bitterly and asked the reason for his sorrow. The man replied, “My name is Han Jaegyeong. My father has died, but I cannot afford to conduct his funeral, nor have I any means of supporting my seventy-year-old mother. That is why I grieve.”

Taking pity on him, Unchi drew a hanging scroll from his sleeve and handed it to him. “Hang this scroll in your house and call for the treasury keeper. If someone answers, tell him to bring you one hundred nyang of silver, and he will do so. Conduct your father’s funeral with that silver. Thereafter, ask for only one nyang of silver each day and use it to support your elderly mother. If you ask for more than that, great trouble will follow. Take great care.”

Half believing and half doubting, Han Jaegyeong asked Unchi his name and where he lived before returning home. When he unrolled the scroll, he saw nothing painted upon it except a large building and a boy standing before it with keys hanging from his waist. By way of experiment, Han Jaegyeong called out, “Treasury keeper!”

Sure enough, a voice answered from within the painting, and the boy walked out of it. Greatly astonished, Han Jaegyeong said, “Bring me one hundred nyang of silver.” The boy brought him one hundred nyang of silver and placed it before him. Han Jaegyeong conducted his father’s funeral with the silver. Thereafter, he called for the treasury keeper every day and lived upon the one nyang of silver he received.

One day, finding himself in need of money, he thought, ‘What harm could there be in borrowing one hundred nyang of silver in advance?’ He called for the treasury keeper and said, “I have need of some money and wish to borrow one hundred nyang of silver in advance.”

The treasury keeper refused. Han Jaegyeong tried to persuade him two or three times, but the treasury keeper gave no answer and entered the painting to open the treasury door. Han Jaegyeong followed him into the painting. When he tried to leave with one hundred nyang of silver, he found that the treasury door had been closed. Alarmed, he called for the treasury keeper, but there was no reply. Han Jaegyeong became furious and kicked the treasury door. At that moment, the Minister of Revenue was attending to his duties in the government office when a treasury keeper reported, “There is the sound of a man’s voice coming from inside the treasury. It is a most peculiar thing.”

Finding this strange, the Minister of Revenue gathered his subordinates and had them open the treasury door. A man was standing there holding silver. The subordinates were greatly astonished and asked, “What manner of thief are you, and how did you get in here?”

Han Jaegyeong replied angrily, “Who are you people, entering another man’s treasury and behaving so insolently?”

The subordinates bound Han Jaegyeong and reported the matter to the Minister of Revenue. The minister had him brought before the stone steps and reprimanded him. Only then did Han Jaegyeong examine his surroundings. He was not in his own house but in a government office. Greatly astonished, he exclaimed, “How have I come to be here? Am I dreaming, or am I awake?” Han Jaegyeong had no idea what had happened.

The Minister of Revenue said, “For entering the treasury and attempting to steal silver, you deserve to die. Tell me who all your accomplices are.”

Han Jaegyeong recounted everything that had happened from beginning to end. When the Minister of Revenue asked where the hanging scroll had come from, Han Jaegyeong told him about Jeon Unchi. The Minister of Revenue asked, “When did you see Jeon Unchi?”

“It has been four or five months since I saw him. He told me that his house was in Namseobu.”

The Minister of Revenue imprisoned Han Jaegyeong and inspected the state treasuries. There was not a single piece of silver, and the treasury was filled with green tree frogs. When another treasury was opened, there was no money inside, only countless yellow snakes lying coiled together. Finding this extraordinary, the Minister of Revenue reported the matter to the king.

Greatly astonished, the king gathered his officials to discuss the matter. Just then, officials from the various storehouses reported, “All the rice in the storehouses has turned into insects and animals.”

The military headquarters also reported, “All the weapons in the armouries have disappeared, and there are only piles of branches.”

The eunuch in charge reported, “The seafood stored in the palace has turned into fish.”

The palace women also reported, “Our jokduri headdresses have turned into golden crows and flown away, and great tigers have entered the inner palace and are harming the palace attendants.”

Greatly alarmed, the king dispatched soldiers armed with bows and crossbows into the inner palace. When the soldiers entered, they found every palace woman riding upon a great tiger. Unable to bring themselves to shoot, they reported the circumstances to the king. The king flew into a rage and commanded, “Shoot the palace women as well!”

The soldiers entered and were about to shoot their arrows together when black clouds suddenly arose. All the palace women riding upon the tigers were enveloped in the clouds and rose into the sky. The king said, “This is all Jeon Unchi’s sorcery. Only when that fellow is apprehended will the kingdom be at peace!”

The Minister of Revenue addressed the king. “The thief we have imprisoned is also an accomplice of Jeon Unchi. Let him be put to death without delay.”

The king granted permission. At the very moment Han Jaegyeong was about to be executed, a violent wind suddenly arose, and Han Jaegyeong vanished without a trace. Unchi had rescued him.

5. Jeon Unchi Enters Royal Service

As Unchi travelled from place to place, he saw the proclamations posted upon the four gates of the city and smiled scornfully. He went before the palace and cried out, “Your servant Jeon Unchi has come of his own accord.”

When the office responsible for transmitting royal commands reported his arrival, the king thought to himself, ‘This fellow’s powers of sorcery are extraordinary, and wherever he goes, he causes mischief. I had better appease him by granting him a government post. Should he cause trouble again, I shall put him to death.’ The king ordered Unchi to be admitted. Unchi entered and prostrated himself before the king, who asked, “Do you know what crimes you have committed?”

Unchi remained prostrate and begged forgiveness. “I deserve to die ten thousand deaths. What can I possibly say in my defence?”

“In recognition of your talents, I pardon your crimes and grant you a government post. You must now serve me with loyalty.”

The king appointed Unchi a royal herald and also granted him the post of Sabok Naeseung, an official responsible for the horses in the royal stables. Unchi expressed his gratitude for the king’s grace, withdrew, established his residence, and served on night duty within the palace.

The royal heralds, military officers responsible for transmitting the king’s commands, subjected the newly appointed Unchi to severe harassment. Following their custom of tormenting newcomers, they took turns beating him. Unchi quietly pulled up a stone pillar standing before a grave and had it receive the blows in his place. Each time the heralds struck him, their palms hurt so much that they could not continue. Thereafter, no one beat Unchi again.

Several months passed, and the royal heralds sent a servant to urge Unchi to hold the customary feast given by a newcomer for his senior colleagues. Unchi said, “Tell them all to come to Baeksajeong Pavilion tomorrow at daybreak.”

The following day, all the royal heralds rode fine horses to Baeksajeong Pavilion. Blue tents rose high into the air, and richly coloured mats were spread on either side. The clear sound of music filled the air, and abundant food had been prepared. It was truly splendid. When everyone had taken their seats, tables laden with food were brought in. As they passed cups of wine among themselves and became half drunk, Unchi said, “You are all enjoying yourselves today, but festivities without women are rather dull. What would you say to bringing the women with whom you were formerly acquainted?”

Delighted in their drunkenness, they said, “We had no idea that our newcomer Jeon possessed such a spirited disposition. Put your talents to use as you see fit.”

Unchi immediately took a servant with him and passed through the southern gate as though flying. The royal heralds said, “If our newcomer Jeon can perform such remarkable feats, he should be able to deal with even the most formidable bandits.” They all praised him.

Before long, Unchi returned with a great number of women and had them wait outside the tent. Another large table laden with food was brought in, and while everyone was enjoying themselves, Unchi stepped forward and said, “In accordance with your wishes, I have brought the women. What would you say to having one seated beside each of you to enliven the festivities?”

Everyone agreed. Unchi first summoned a woman and seated her before the chief of the royal heralds, saying, “Do not leave your place, and attend to him diligently.”

In this manner, he seated the women one by one. The women, however, were all wives of the royal heralds. Afraid that the others might recognise them, the heralds could not utter a word. Though inwardly furious, they could not show their anger. At last, they had the tables removed, brought out their horses, and hurried home. Their servants had no idea what had happened and found the matter exceedingly strange.

When the royal heralds returned to their respective houses, they found every household in turmoil. Some people were running to deliver urgent news, others were rushing to apothecaries to obtain cheongsimhwan pills to calm the mind, others were summoning physicians to administer acupuncture, and still others were weeping aloud. When the heralds asked what had happened, they were all told that their wives had died.

When the royal herald Kim returned home, a maidservant said, “Your wife suddenly passed away earlier today while cutting some cloth.”

Kim became furious and said, “That woman was acting as a gisaeng at the feast at Baeksajeong Pavilion, conspiring with that wretch Jeon to humiliate me before everyone! How can a woman of a scholar-official’s family conduct herself in such a manner? Now I can no longer hold office, and the honour of my household is ruined. How can I express my indignation?”

Suddenly, the maidservant came running back and said, “Your wife is waking up!”

Kim put aside his anger and hurried into the inner quarters. His wife sat up and said, “I dozed off for a moment earlier today, and a man dressed in a red robe seized me and dragged me away without any regard for right or wrong. A servant dressed in yellow rushed towards me, covered me with a jangot cloak, and put me upon a horse. When we arrived at a certain place, I saw countless women like myself. While I was wondering what to do, that fellow called Royal Herald Jeon seated me before you and told me to attend to you diligently. He seated the other women one by one in the same manner. The royal heralds sat in a row and were served with food, when suddenly you rose with an angry expression, mounted your horse, and went home. The others likewise departed in great anger, without a thought for their wives. I was led about from place to place with the other women, not knowing what to do. When I awoke, I found that it had all been a dream. Yet the people of our household are weeping aloud, thinking that I have died. How can there be such a strange occurrence in this world?” Upon hearing this, Kim was at a loss for words.

Unable to contain their indignation, the royal heralds said, “That treacherous and wicked wretch Jeon Unchi has entered the royal court and subjected us to such humiliation. When shall we kill him and satisfy our resentment?”

Having deceived all the royal heralds, Jeon Unchi returned home and thought to himself, ‘Though I committed offences against the kingdom for which I deserved to die, I was pardoned and even granted a government post. The king’s grace knows no bounds. I must now repent of my former misdeeds and serve him with loyalty.’

Thereafter, Unchi applied himself to his duties and cared diligently for the horses in the royal stables. The horses grew fat and suffered no illness, and the royal court regarded Unchi with admiration.

Meanwhile, on Mount Gadal there lived a man named Yeom Jun. His courage surpassed that of other men, and he excelled in the martial arts. He gathered several thousand bandits, established a mountain stronghold, plundered villages, attacked various districts, seized weapons, money and grain, and killed people. Many districts were consequently thrown into turmoil.

When the provincial governor reported these circumstances to the royal court, the king became deeply concerned and gathered his officials to discuss the matter. “The bandits have grown so powerful. Who among you is capable of destroying them?” No one dared answer.

Suddenly, a man stepped forward from among the officials and said, “The grace I have received from Your Majesty knows no bounds. Though I possess little talent, I wish to cut off Yeom Jun’s head and relieve Your Majesty of your troubles.” The king looked and saw that it was Jeon Unchi.

Greatly pleased, the king asked his officials, “What do you think?” They all replied that it would be proper.

The king asked, “How many soldiers and horses shall I provide you with?”

“As the enemy is said to be powerful, I believe it would be best for me to go alone and assess the enemy’s strength and situation before mobilising the troops.”

The king granted his request, presented him with the seal of his office and a sword, and gave him authority to issue commands at his own discretion. Unchi thanked the king for his grace and withdrew from the court. The following night, he rode upon a cloud to Namseobu to see his mother and told her that he had received the king’s command to assess the enemy’s strength and situation.

His mother cautioned him. “It is exceedingly dangerous to venture recklessly into enemy territory without knowing the enemy’s true situation. Take the utmost care and do not disappoint the hopes of your king and your parents.”

Heeding his mother’s words, Unchi returned to the capital. At daybreak, he set out with some ten officers of the Police Bureau.

6. Yeom Jun of Mount Gadal

Unchi arrived at the chief government office of the province and had the officers remain there. Taking only the seal of his office and his sword, he set out alone. He shook his body, transformed himself into an eagle, and entered Mount Gadal.

Yeom Jun was riding majestically upon a white horse beneath a ceremonial parasol. With beautiful women dressed in splendid garments and red skirts on either side of him, and more than a hundred followers in attendance, he was out hunting. Suddenly, Yeom Jun issued a command. “Today, the warriors who went out to the various provinces will return. Slaughter ten large oxen tomorrow and prepare a feast.”

Unchi examined Yeom Jun. He had a magnificent frame and a bright complexion, his eyes were like bells, and his beard resembled needles bundled together and set upright. He was truly a hero of his age. Unchi suddenly devised a plan. He stripped leaves from the trees and transformed them into soldiers by means of his magical arts. He armed them with spears and swords, had them unfurl their banners, and established a strong military camp. Unchi put on a helmet adorned with two phoenixes and dressed himself in splendid battle attire. Holding the sword bestowed upon him by the king, he mounted a black horse and forced his way through the entrance to the valley. The fortress gate was firmly closed. Unchi recited an incantation, and the gate opened of its own accord. Riding through it, he looked to either side and saw splendid houses standing in rows. It was a splendid and bustling sight.

After examining his surroundings, Unchi transformed himself into an eagle again and entered the rear garden. Yeom Jun was seated upon a golden chair, with his commanders seated on either side of him. In the hall behind them, several hundred beautiful women sat in rows, receiving cups of wine.

Wishing to observe their movements, Unchi recited an incantation. Countless eagles descended, covering the sky, seized all the tables laden with food before the people, and rose into the air. A violent wind arose, scattering sand and sending stones rolling about. Greatly alarmed, the people could not open their eyes and were swept off their feet by the wind. Tents and all manner of objects were carried upwards into the air. Yeom Jun clung desperately to the base of a tree upon a hill, while his soldiers, still holding rice cakes and meat in their hands, were swept away by the wind and went tumbling about. Some even vomited liquid excrement. The commotion continued from morning until midday. Yeom Jun, his commanders and his soldiers had scarcely recovered their senses when white snow suddenly began to fall in torrents. In an instant, the snow accumulated to a depth of more than ten gil. The people could not even open their eyes and did not know what to do. Then the wind suddenly ceased. Not a single drop of melted snow remained.

Yeom Jun came out into the main hall, rang a large bell, and summoned his commanders and soldiers. While they were discussing this strange calamity, a soldier guarding the gate came running in to report. “A commander is leading his troops through the eastern gate after breaking it down!”

Greatly alarmed, Yeom Jun hastened his troops into formation, had their banners put in order, and advanced to the front of his camp. He mounted his horse and rode forward with a spear in his hand. Unchi cried out in a loud voice, “What manner of wretch are you, that you should rely upon your strength and cruelty, gather followers in the mountains, invade districts and kill the people? I shall apprehend every rat among you and uphold the laws of the kingdom. If you value your life, surrender at once and obey the command of Heaven!”

Yeom Jun became furious and replied sharply, “I uphold the will of Heaven and follow the wishes of the people. I intend to overthrow the tyrannical king and rescue the people from their misery. How dare you attempt to stop me?”

As soon as he had spoken, he charged. Their spear and sword met, and the two exchanged blows several dozen times. The blade of Yeom Jun’s spear obscured the sunlight, while the gleam of Unchi’s sword formed a rainbow in the air. It was truly like two tigers contending for prey upon a deserted mountain and two dragons contending for a wish-granting pearl in the blue sea. The two commanders fought with ever greater vigour, but neither prevailed. As darkness fell, gongs sounded from both camps, and the troops withdrew.

When Yeom Jun returned to his camp, his commanders said, “Yesterday, we were alarmed by a strange calamity, yet today you have successfully confronted a commander as fierce as a tiger. Heaven must be assisting you. The courage of the enemy commander is also exceptional. General, you must not underestimate him.”

Yeom Jun laughed and said, “Though the enemy commander is courageous, why should I fear him? Tomorrow, I shall surely capture Unchi and proceed directly to the capital.”

The following day, the gates of the camp were opened, and Yeom Jun came out and cried in a loud voice, “Jeon Unchi, come forth and meet my blade! Today we shall determine the victor!”

Yeom Jun charged, driving his horse from side to side. Unchi likewise became furious, spurred his horse forward, brandished his sword, and attacked Yeom Jun directly. They exchanged blows more than thirty times, but there was not the slightest opening in Yeom Jun’s spearmanship. Unchi thought to himself, ‘I cannot prevail against Yeom Jun in the martial arts.’ He shook his body, and his real body rose into the air while he had his false body continue fighting Yeom Jun. He then cried out, “I have never taken a life, but now that you defy the command of Heaven, I am compelled to kill you. Do not bear me a grudge!” He raised his sword to strike Yeom Jun, then thought again, ‘How can I suddenly take a life? I ought to capture this fellow alive.’

Unchi rose into the air, flashed his sword, and cried out, “Behold my powers!”

Greatly astonished, Yeom Jun looked up towards the sky and saw lightning flashing among a bank of clouds. It was not lightning but the gleam of Unchi’s sword. Yeom Jun was so alarmed that the colour drained from his face. He tried to return to his camp, but Unchi stood before him, blocking his way with a sword. Unchi was also pursuing him from behind. On his left and right, Unchi closed in upon him. Above his head, Unchi was riding a cloud, brandishing his sword and aiming it at Yeom Jun’s head. Yeom Jun became so bewildered that he fell from his horse.

Unchi descended from the clouds, had the false Unchi command the troops, bound Yeom Jun, and sent him back to the main camp. Unchi himself rode forward and attacked the enemy camp. Seeing that Yeom Jun had been captured, the enemy commanders and soldiers bound their hands and surrendered. Unchi had them brought before the stone steps and admonished them. “You have aided a traitor and defied the command of Heaven. Though you deserve to die ten thousand deaths, I grant you a special pardon. Return to your native villages, devote yourselves to farming, and become law-abiding subjects.”

The enemy commanders lowered their heads, bowed twice, and dispersed to their homes. It was like the occasion in ancient times when Zhang Zifang sang sorrowful songs of home beneath the autumn moon on Mount Jiming, and the men of Jiangdong, longing for their native land, dispersed. Unchi entered Yeom Jun’s private quarters, released the several hundred beautiful women, and allowed each of them to return home. He then returned to his camp and seated himself upon the command platform. He ordered the soldiers on either side to bring Yeom Jun before him and reprimanded him in a stern voice. “With such exceptional talents and courage, you ought to have served the king loyally and brought honour to your family for generations. Yet you dared entertain thoughts of rebellion and threw the kingdom into turmoil. How can such crimes be forgiven?” Unchi ordered his warriors to take Yeom Jun outside the camp gates and behead him.

Yeom Jun pleaded sorrowfully, “My crimes deserve the extermination of three branches of my family, but if you, General, will exercise the virtue of sparing life, I shall surely mend my ways and follow you.”

“If you will truly correct your former misdeeds and become a good man, would that not be a fine thing?”

Unchi ordered his warriors to release Yeom Jun from his bonds. He comforted him with kind words, sent him back to his native village, and withdrew his troops.

The king summoned Unchi and asked how he had defeated the enemy. Unchi recounted everything in detail from beginning to end, and the king praised him repeatedly and bestowed many rewards upon him.

After Unchi returned to the capital, all the officials of the royal court came to congratulate him upon his achievements. Among the royal heralds, however, not a single man came. They still bore him a grudge for the humiliation they had suffered at the feast at Baeksajeong Pavilion. Unchi decided to play another trick upon them.

One night, during the fourth watch, the moon shone brightly, and there was not a single cloud in the blue sky. Riding clouds of five colours, Unchi gathered mighty spirits wearing yellow headcloths and all manner of dokkaebi. He summoned a spirit general and commanded him, “Go at once and apprehend all the royal heralds.”

The spirit general departed upon receiving the command and presently returned with all the royal heralds. Unchi seated himself upon a chair made of clouds and arranged the spirit generals in rows on either side of him. Lamps and candles shone brilliantly. Unchi cried out in a stern voice, “Where are the spirits wearing yellow headcloths? Bring in all the prisoners!”

The spirits obeyed at once and dragged the prisoners in one by one. Terrified, the royal heralds prostrated themselves. When they raised their heads, they saw the king of ghosts and the spirit generals arrayed on either side, their bearing exceedingly solemn and majestic. Unchi reprimanded them in a loud voice. “It is true that I briefly subjected your wives to humiliation some time ago in order to play a trick upon you, but why do you bear me such resentment and treat me with such neglect? I have long intended to apprehend you and send you to hell, but at night I have been attending to my duties in Heaven, and by day I have been occupied with the affairs of the kingdom. Thus I have postponed the matter until now. I am now compelled to send you to Pungdo Prison in the underworld, where you shall suffer and pay for the crime of treating others with contempt.” Having spoken, he summoned a spirit and commanded him, “Escort these prisoners to Pungdo Prison and deliver them to King Yama. Confine them in hell, and after the immense span of eighty thousand kalpas, let them be reborn as beasts.”

Upon hearing these words, all the royal heralds trembled even more violently and felt as though their souls were leaving their bodies. They pleaded sorrowfully, “In our foolishness and ignorance, we have committed an offence. We beg you to remember the fellowship of those who serve together in royal office and forgive our crimes.”

Unchi thought for a long while and said, “I ought to send you to Pungdo and make you suffer, but in consideration of our former acquaintance, I shall grant you a special pardon on this occasion. I shall decide what to do according to your conduct hereafter.” He then turned to the spirits and commanded, “Drive them all out!”

The royal heralds suddenly awoke. It had all been a dream. Sweat streamed from their bodies, soaking their bedding and pillows, and their minds were in a daze.

Thereafter, all the royal heralds gathered at their government office and spoke of the dreams they had experienced that night. Every one of them told exactly the same story, as though it had come from a single mouth. From that time onwards, the royal heralds treated Unchi with exceptional care and courtesy.

7. The False Accusation of Treason

One day, the king asked the Minister of Revenue, “You told me some time ago that all the silver and money in the Ministry of Revenue had turned into other things. What is their condition now?”

“They remain exactly as they were before.”

As the king was deeply concerned, Unchi stepped forward from among the officials and said, “I wish to examine closely the strange occurrences in the various treasuries and report my findings to Your Majesty.” The king granted his request.

Unchi immediately accompanied the Minister of Revenue to the ministry and opened a treasury door. The silver was piled up just as it had been before. Greatly astonished, the Minister of Revenue said, “When I inspected the treasury yesterday, it was filled with nothing but green tree frogs. In the course of a single night, they have turned back into silver. It is a truly extraordinary thing.”

When the other treasuries were opened, everything was likewise as it had been before. The weapons in the various military headquarters were also in their former places. Everyone was astonished and marvelled at what had happened. Unchi reported his findings to the king exactly as he had observed them. The king was pleased, though he suspected that the previous disturbances had been caused by Unchi’s sorcery. At that moment, an official charged with remonstrating with the king stepped forward and said, “It is said that four or five men in the Hoseo region have gathered together to plot a rebellion. A man came to me with documents denouncing them. I have detained him and now report the matter to Your Majesty.”

The king said, “My virtue is insufficient, and bandits rise up here and there. How lamentable it is!” He ordered the Royal Tribunal and the Police Bureau to apprehend them. The prisoners were soon brought before him, and the king questioned them personally.

One of them said, “We intended to make Jeon Unchi our king and bring peace to the people, but now that our plot has been discovered, we deserve to die ten thousand deaths.”

Unchi was standing beside the king as one of the officials charged with questioning the prisoners when his name unexpectedly appeared in the testimony of the traitor. The king flew into a rage and said, “I knew that Jeon Unchi would eventually plot a rebellion, and now his name has appeared in the testimony of a traitor!”

The king immediately ordered Unchi to be seized and the instruments of punishment prepared. He reprimanded him, saying, “I pardoned your former crimes and even granted you a government post. Yet instead of being grateful for the grace of the kingdom, you have now committed treason. Die without making excuses!” He then sternly commanded the guards, “Kill him with a single blow!”

A guard attempted to strike Unchi with all his strength, but his arm hurt so much that he could not even raise the rod. Unchi addressed the king. “For the crimes I have committed thus far, I deserve to die ten thousand deaths, but I have truly been wronged in this accusation of treason.” He thought to himself, ‘Someone must have plotted against me for such a thing to happen. How grievous it is!’ He addressed the king again. “If I die now, I shall be unable to pass on to the world the skills I have acquired throughout my life. I prostrate myself before Your Majesty and beg you to grant my last wish.”

The king thought, ‘This fellow possesses exceedingly remarkable skills. I shall put him to the test.’ He asked, “What skills do you possess?”

“I am skilled in painting. When I paint a tree, it gradually grows. When I paint an animal, it walks about. When I paint a mountain, grass and trees grow upon it. People call my paintings masterpieces. If I die without leaving these paintings to the world, I shall become a restless spirit.”

The king thought, ‘If this fellow dies and becomes a restless spirit, he will cause us trouble.’ He immediately had Unchi released from his bonds and provided him with a brush, ink and paper.

Unchi took up the brush and began painting a landscape. He painted countless valleys and peaks, and from the summit of a high mountain he made a waterfall ten thousand gil in length descend. Beside a stream, he painted the hanging branches of a willow, and beneath them a saddled donkey. Unchi cast aside his brush and bowed four times before the king.

The king asked, “You are a criminal condemned to death. What is the meaning of these four bows?”

“I now bid Your Majesty farewell and enter the mountains.”

Unchi mounted the donkey in the painting. Riding the donkey, he entered the mountains in the painting and suddenly vanished without a trace. The king flew into a rage and said, “I have been deceived by this fellow again. What am I to do henceforth?”

The king ordered the officials beside him to burn the painting. The other prisoners were again subjected to severe questioning, and after their confessions had been obtained, they were all taken out and beheaded. Bitterly resentful at having been deceived by Unchi, the king issued an order to every province, promising a reward of a thousand geum and a government post to anyone who apprehended Jeon Unchi and brought him before the court.

Through his magical arts, Unchi had deceived the king and escaped death. He returned home and told his mother everything that had happened from beginning to end. Greatly alarmed, she said, “From now on, conceal yourself and never return to the royal court. You have deceived the king, and such a crime cannot be tolerated between Heaven and Earth. With what face will you meet your ancestors after your death?” She reprimanded Unchi severely. Having received his mother’s reprimand, Unchi remained in the mountains and quietly devoted himself to reading. From time to time, he rode a donkey and admired the scenery of mountains and rivers.

One day, Unchi came upon a young monk leading a beautiful woman into the mountains. Before long, the woman climbed a tree and attempted to hang herself. Unchi happened to be returning to the mountains after buying and drinking some wine at a village house. Greatly alarmed at the sight, he hurried towards her, freed her from her bonds, and rubbed her hands and feet until she regained consciousness. When Unchi asked what had happened, the woman said, “The monk who passed by earlier was a close acquaintance of my husband while he was alive. I lost my husband early in life and have lived in chastity ever since. Today happens to be the anniversary of my husband’s death. The monk came to me and persuaded me to accompany him to his temple to perform a Buddhist memorial rite for my husband. As he earnestly entreated me to go with him, I trusted him and followed without suspicion. Yet that man, harbouring wicked intentions, brought me here and raped me. What reason have I to live now? That is why I tried to take my own life.”

Unchi comforted the woman and sent her home before returning to the mountains. There was a large hermitage where he found the monk he had seen the previous day. Unchi quietly recited an incantation and breathed magical energy into him. The monk’s appearance changed into that of Jeon Unchi. Leaving the monk at the temple, Unchi watched his movements.

An official who was travelling about in search of bandits happened to visit the temple. Seeing the monk and believing him to be Jeon Unchi, he hurriedly reported the matter to the magistrate. Greatly pleased, the magistrate dispatched soldiers to apprehend the monk, bind him and escort him to the capital. The king immediately prepared to question him personally. The office responsible for transmitting royal commands then reported, “Altogether, three hundred and sixty-one men believed to be Jeon Unchi have been apprehended and brought here from the various provinces and districts. This must surely be the work of Jeon Unchi’s sorcery.”

The king was furious and did not know how to deal with the matter. Wang Yeonhui, the Chief Royal Secretary, who headed the office responsible for receiving and transmitting royal commands, addressed the king. “Jeon Unchi’s powers of sorcery are beyond comprehension. I fear that he may escape us again. Without attempting to distinguish the true Unchi from the false ones, let them all be beheaded.”

The king agreed, proceeded to Sipjagak Pavilion, and ordered all the men bearing the likeness of Jeon Unchi to be brought in and beheaded in turn. One of them stepped forward and said, “I am not Jeon Unchi. I am Wang Yeonhui, the Chief Royal Secretary.”

The king examined him and saw that he was unmistakably Wang Yeonhui. Finding this strange, he questioned the officials on either side of him. They replied, “That man is Jeon Unchi.”

The king sighed. “Such is the ill fortune of our kingdom that this creature makes sport of us to such an extent. How are we to preserve the royal household and the kingdom? In attempting to kill a single traitor, we shall kill countless innocent officials and wronged subjects.” At last, the king suspended the questioning.

At that time, Unchi was practising his magical arts from within the clouds. He transformed himself into the likeness of Wang Yeonhui and came out through the palace gate. Wang Yeonhui’s servants were waiting with a horse ready for him. They escorted Unchi to Wang Yeonhui’s house. Unchi went straight into the inner quarters and conversed with Wang Yeonhui’s wife. Neither she nor anyone in the household had the slightest idea that he was Jeon Unchi.

Presently, the real Wang Yeonhui came out of the palace and looked for his servants, but not a single one was there. Finding this strange, he borrowed a horse and servants from a fellow official. When he rode home, he found his servants standing before the gate. Greatly angered, he demanded an explanation.

The servants said, “We escorted you home earlier. How is it that you are here again, my lord?” They merely looked at one another.

Puzzled, Wang Yeonhui entered the inner quarters. The maidservants clapped their hands and exclaimed, “What is the meaning of this? Our master came home earlier and is already inside. How can this be?”

The maidservants all began talking at once. Still unable to understand what had happened, Wang Yeonhui entered his bedroom. Sure enough, another Wang Yeonhui was sitting there, engrossed in conversation with his wife. Wang Yeonhui flew into a rage and reprimanded him. “What manner of wretch are you, that you dare enter the household of a scholar-official and converse with my wife?” He shouted at his servants, “Bind that fellow at once!”

Unchi likewise exclaimed, “What manner of wretch are you, that you should assume my appearance, enter my inner quarters and attempt to rape my wife? How can there be such an outrage in this world?” Unchi shouted at the servants, “Drive that fellow out at once!”

Looking at the two men, the servants could not distinguish the real one from the false one. While they stood there not knowing what to do, Unchi spoke with authority. “I have heard that strange creatures cannot retain a human appearance for long.”

Unchi spat water towards Wang Yeonhui, then took out some powdered cinnabar, a red mineral, and applied it to him. Wang Yeonhui’s appearance changed, and he became a fox with nine tails. Only then did the servants rush forward with swords and clubs, intending to beat the fox to death. Unchi restrained them, saying, “This is a grave calamity and must be reported to the authorities. For the time being, bind it securely, confine it in a room, and guard it well.”

Obeying his command, the servants bound Wang Yeonhui and locked him away. Overcome by this unexpected calamity, Wang Yeonhui attempted to speak, but only the cries of a fox came from his mouth. His mind was in a daze, and he could do nothing but lie there shedding tears. Though outwardly a beast, inwardly he was truly a man. Unchi thought, ‘If I continue this deception for another two or three days, he will not survive.’

That night, during the fourth watch, Unchi went to Wang Yeonhui and said, “There was no enmity between you and me, yet you were determined to kill me and gain merit in the service of the kingdom. I too wished to kill you first and satisfy my resentment, but as I have never taken a life, I shall forgive you. You must never conduct yourself in such a manner again.”

Unchi recited an incantation, and the fox returned to the appearance of Wang Yeonhui. Only then did Wang Yeonhui realise that his misfortune had been caused by Unchi’s magical arts. He hastily said, “Not knowing the greatness of your talents, I have committed an error.” Wang Yeonhui begged forgiveness countless times.

Unchi gave him further instructions. “I have restored you and shall now depart. After I leave, there will be a commotion in your household. When that happens, deal with it in such and such a manner.” Unchi returned to Namseobu.

Wang Yeonhui immediately summoned his servants and said, “Go and see exactly what has become of that creature.”

When the servants went to the room, the creature had vanished without a trace. Greatly astonished, they reported what they had found. Pretending to be angry, Wang Yeonhui said, “You have failed to guard it properly and allowed it to escape!” He reprimanded his servants repeatedly and dismissed them.

Unchi returned to the hermitage. The monk still bore the likeness of Jeon Unchi. Unchi spat water towards him and recited an incantation, whereupon the monk returned to his former appearance. Unchi reprimanded him severely. “Having become a monk, you ought to honour the teachings of the Buddha. Yet you enticed a woman who had lost her husband and lived in chastity, raped her, and drove her to the point of taking her own life. Even ten thousand deaths would not atone for your crime. I intended to have you put to death by giving you the appearance of Jeon Unchi, but I could not bring myself to take a life. I therefore spare you and restore your former appearance. Never commit such an act again.”

8. The Woman in the Painting

As Unchi was returning home, he came upon several young men who were competing with one another to examine a hanging scroll and praising it highly. “The painting on this scroll is a masterpiece unrivalled in the world.”

Unchi approached and saw that it was a painting of a beautiful woman. She held a child in her arms and was amusing it. Her lips seemed to speak, and her eyes seemed to gaze. The painting was full of life. Unchi devised a plan and said with a smile, “What makes this painting such a masterpiece that you praise it so extravagantly?”

Among them was a scholar named Oh, who replied, “Perhaps you speak this way because your standards are high, but do not speak without reason. Her lips seem to speak, and her eyes seem to gaze. How can it not be a masterpiece?”

Unchi smiled and asked the price of the painting. Oh replied, “Fifty nyang of silver. Considering the quality of the painting, the price is rather low.”

“I too have a hanging scroll. Have a look at it.”

Unchi drew from his sleeve a painting of a beautiful woman and laid it before them. The woman in the painting was exceedingly beautiful. She wore a green jacket and a red skirt, with a floral crown upon her head. She was truly a beauty without equal, as lovely as a peony. The people examined the painting and praised it. “This painting too is full of life, much like our own scroll.”

Unchi smiled scornfully and said, “Your scroll may be considered a fine painting, but in its lifelikeness, it cannot compare with this one. Observe the quality of this painting.” Unchi hung up the scroll and called softly, “Where is the celestial maiden of wine?”

Suddenly, the beautiful woman in the painting answered and walked out of it, accompanied by a boy. Unchi said, “Pour some wine for these gentlemen.”

The celestial maiden of wine answered and filled their cups. Unchi drank first, followed by the others in turn. The wine tasted exceedingly sweet and refreshing. When everyone had drunk their fill, the celestial maiden of wine cleared away the table, returned to the painting, and became a painting again. Greatly astonished, the scholars said to one another, “This is neither a wonder wrought in the heavenly realm nor a trick played in a dream. It is a treasure rarely seen throughout the ages!”

Oh said, “I shall put it to the test myself.” He then asked Unchi, “Our wine is not very good. May I call the celestial maiden of wine and ask her for some more?”

Unchi granted his request. Oh called softly to the celestial maiden of wine and said, “Our wine is not very good, and I wish to drink more of yours.”

Suddenly, the celestial maiden of wine answered and came out carrying a wine flask. The boy followed her, carrying a table. The celestial maiden of wine tilted the flask and poured the wine. Oh drank first, and the other scholars each drank a cup in turn. They rose from their seats and expressed their gratitude. “How fortunate we are to have met you today, to have tasted such excellent wine, and to have witnessed such a remarkable thing.”

Unchi said, “Though the painting on this scroll is full of life, it is of little use. Besides, you have merely drunk wine from a painting. What is there to thank me for?”

Oh said, “If the scroll is of no use to you, why not sell it to me before you leave?”

“If someone truly wishes to possess it, I am willing to sell it.”

When Oh asked the price, Unchi replied, “The woman holding the wine flask is the celestial maiden responsible for wine in the heavenly realm. Her wine will never run dry in a lifetime, and the scroll may therefore be regarded as an exceedingly precious treasure. I wish to receive one thousand nyang of silver for it.”

“I shall not dispute whether the price is high or low. My friend, why do you not come to my house first?”

Unchi agreed and accompanied Oh to his house. Handing him the scroll, he said, “I shall return tomorrow. Have the money ready.” Having said this, Unchi departed.

Oh, who was exceedingly drunk, hung the scroll upon a wall in the outer quarters. When he examined it, the celestial maiden of wine was standing there holding her wine flask. Oh was captivated by her beautiful figure. He took her jade-like hand, seated her upon his lap, and, unable to contain his affection for her, was about to take her to his bed. At that very moment, the door opened, and someone came rushing in. It was Oh’s wife, Lady Min.

In matters of jealousy, Lady Min was in the vanguard; in matters of envy, she was a commander. Such was her disposition that even the affairs of others would send her rushing out with a knife. That night, when she saw Oh sporting with a woman, Lady Min flew into a rage and tried to strike the celestial maiden of wine, but the maiden was already a painting again. Lady Min grew even more furious, seized the scroll and tore it to pieces. Greatly alarmed, Oh said, “I agreed to pay one thousand nyang of silver for someone else’s scroll. What do you intend to do when its owner comes?”

“When its owner comes, I shall thoroughly humiliate him!”

While the two were quarrelling incessantly, Unchi happened to arrive. Oh welcomed him and explained what had happened. Upon hearing the story, Unchi decided to play a trick upon Lady Min and used his magical arts to give her the outward form of a large golden snake. Inwardly, Lady Min remained a human being, but outwardly she was a great snake. When she tried to speak, no words came out, and when she tried to rise, she could not move her body. Unchi said to Oh, “I left the scroll here for your sake, and now the treasure has been destroyed. It was unfortunate that I met you. Moreover, a grave calamity will befall your household. You must be careful.”

“What sort of calamity is to befall us?”

Unchi said, “There is a creature a thousand years old in your household. It will repeatedly assume your wife’s appearance and bring calamity upon you.”

“Why would such a creature do such a thing?”

“Your wife tore my scroll to pieces, and she will turn into a strange creature and cause mischief. Open the door to her room and see for yourself.”

Disbelieving him, Oh opened the door. Sure enough, Lady Min was nowhere to be seen, and a great snake three bal in length lay there. Greatly alarmed, Oh came out with a pale face and said to Unchi, “There is indeed a great snake inside. I wish to kill it.”

Unchi restrained him. “That creature is a spiritual being a thousand years old. If you kill it, a great misfortune will follow. If I tie a talisman around the snake’s waist, it will disappear of its own accord tonight.”

Unchi took out a talisman, tied it around the snake’s waist, and cautioned Oh, “Do not open the door to the room.”

Unchi departed, waited until daybreak, and returned to Oh’s house. Seeing Lady Min, he reprimanded her. “You have treated your husband with contempt, habitually behaved with cruelty, and taken pleasure in jealousy. You have even torn another man’s scroll to pieces and insulted me. For these offences, I intend to give you the form of a great golden snake, confine you in a rocky cave, and make you suffer. If you now undertake to mend your ways, I shall remove that form from you.” Lady Min nodded. Unchi recited an incantation, and the form of the great golden snake fell away of its own accord. Lady Min hurriedly rose and bowed countless times in gratitude.

On his way home, Unchi visited a man named Yang Bongan, with whom he had studied in former days. Yang Bongan was lying ill. Alarmed, Unchi questioned him closely about the symptoms of his illness. Yang said, “My chest and stomach ache, and for a long time I have been unable either to eat or to drink. I fear that I shall not recover.”

Unchi felt his pulse and said, “This illness has arisen from thinking of someone. On whose account have you become so ill?”

“That is indeed the case. It is on account of a woman named Jeong who lives in Haehyeondong, inside the southern gate. She is beautiful enough to bring a kingdom to ruin and lost her husband early in life. Her house is next to my uncle’s, and by chance I saw her over the wall. Ever since then, my longing for her has grown more ardent with each passing day, until my illness has reached this state. I fear I shall not remain in this world for long.”

“Send an eloquent matchmaker to propose marriage to her.”

Yang said, “Her determination to preserve her chastity is exceptional. I fear that not only would the marriage fail to come about, but I would also suffer humiliation.”

“Then what would you say, my friend, if I brought the woman to you?”

“However extraordinary your talents may be, you will not be able to bring that woman here. Do not entertain such thoughts in vain, my friend.”

“Do not worry, my friend.” Having said this, Unchi rode upon a cloud and departed.

9. Lovesickness

Lady Jeong had lost her husband early in life and grieved day and night, wishing to die. As she had an elderly mother and no other siblings, the mother and daughter passed their days relying upon each other.

One day, unable to compose herself, Lady Jeong was pacing about her room. Suddenly, an immortal appeared from among the clouds. He wore a red outer robe and a belt adorned with jade, a golden crown upon his head, and held a ceremonial tablet made of jade in his hand. In a clear and resonant voice, he cried out, “Lady Jeong, mistress of this household, come forth and hear the command of the Jade Emperor!”

Upon hearing this, Lady Jeong informed her mother, who was astonished and found the matter exceedingly strange. She hurriedly prepared an altar for burning incense in the main hall. Lady Jeong descended into the courtyard and prostrated herself. Unchi said, “Celestial maiden of letters! How have you enjoyed your time in the human world? You are now to attend the Peach Banquet at Yaochi in the heavenly realm.”

Greatly astonished upon hearing the command of the Jade Emperor, Lady Jeong said, “I possess an impure body of the human world and am also guilty of offences. How could I possibly ascend to the heavenly realm?”

“The celestial maiden of letters has drunk the impure waters of the human world and forgotten the affairs of Heaven.”

Unchi filled a cup with fragrant wine from a gourd flask and had an attendant boy offer it to her. Lady Jeong drank it, became dazed, and presently lost consciousness. Unchi enveloped her in clouds and rose into the air. Lady Jeong’s mother bowed countless times towards Heaven and offered her congratulations.

At that time, Young Master Gangnim was gathering beggars and travelling through the marketplace, begging for food. Suddenly, a fragrance filled the air, and clouds of five colours moved towards the southeast. Young Master Gangnim raised his head, looked up, and lifted his hand to point once at the clouds. A gate in the clouds opened of its own accord, and an immortal and a beautiful woman fell to the ground.

It was Jeon Unchi. He had been carrying Lady Jeong through the air upon a cloud when a dark vapour suddenly rose into the sky, undoing his magical arts and causing him to fall to the ground. Greatly astonished, Unchi looked about him, but there was nothing to be seen. Finding this strange, he was about to perform his magical arts again when a beggar boy suddenly appeared and reprimanded him loudly. “Hear me, you wretched Jeon Unchi! You have learnt sorcery, deceived Heaven, and now seek to dishonour a woman who preserves her chastity. How could Heaven remain indifferent? For this reason, I have been commanded to kill a fellow such as you. Do not bear me a grudge!”

Unchi flew into a rage, drew the sword he was wearing, and attempted to threaten the boy. The sword, however, turned into a white fox and attempted to harm Unchi himself. Finding this strange, Unchi tried to evade it, but his feet were fixed to the ground, and he could not move. He hurriedly attempted to transform himself, but his magical arts had no effect. Greatly astonished, Unchi examined the boy closely. Though his clothes were ragged, Unchi realised that his magical arts surpassed his own. He bowed and begged forgiveness. “Though I have eyes, they have no pupils, and I have failed to recognise you, Master. I deserve to die ten thousand deaths. I have an elderly mother, and my household was so poor that I could not support her. I was therefore compelled to deceive the king. The second time I deceived him was to preserve my own life. Today, I attempted to compromise Lady Jeong’s chastity in order to save a sick friend. I beg you, Master, to forgive my offences and teach me the Way of the immortals.”

“Even without your telling me, I knew all this already. Such is the ill fortune of the kingdom that men such as yourself practise sorcery recklessly and cause disturbances. I ought to kill you. In consideration of your elderly mother’s circumstances, I shall spare your life for now. Take Lady Jeong back to her house at once and devise a good plan to save Yang. There is someone who may take Lady Jeong’s place. She lost her parents early in life, has no one upon whom to rely, and is exceedingly poor, but she is kind-hearted. Her surname is also Jeong, and she is twenty-four years old. Should you disobey my words, a great calamity will befall you.”

Unchi thanked him and said, “May I ask your honoured name, Master?”

“I am Young Master Gangnim. I travel from place to place, making sport of the world.” Young Master Gangnim released Unchi so that he could practise his magical arts again.

Unchi immediately took Lady Jeong back to her house. From the air, he called loudly to her mother. “When I ascended to the palace of the Jade Emperor earlier, the Emperor said that the celestial maiden of letters had not yet cleansed herself of all her offences. He commanded me to return her to the human world and bring her back after she had endured further hardships. I have therefore brought her home. See that your daughter cultivates goodness in her heart.” Unchi took out a fragrant medicine and placed it in Lady Jeong’s mouth. Presently, she awoke and regained her senses.

Unchi returned to Young Master Gangnim and asked where the other woman lived. Young Master Gangnim gave him a Hwan-yeongdan pill capable of changing a person’s face and told him where to find the woman’s house. Unchi took his leave and went there. In a dilapidated, single-room thatched cottage, a woman sat alone, deep in sorrow. Unchi approached and comforted her. “I already know of your difficult circumstances. I pity you for having passed the age of twenty-one without marrying and for living in such solitude. I wish to arrange a marriage for you.”

The woman lowered her head in embarrassment. Unchi gave her the Hwan-yeongdan pill, spat water towards her, and recited an incantation. Her face became unmistakably identical to that of Lady Jeong. Unchi told the woman about Yang’s illness and what had happened when he took Lady Jeong away, and instructed her on how she should conduct herself thereafter. He covered her face with a cloth and rode upon a cloud to Yang’s house. Leaving the woman in the outer quarters, Unchi entered the inner quarters and said to Yang, “Lady Jeong’s chastity is indeed so exemplary that I dared not even broach the subject. I have returned empty-handed.”

Yang sighed sorrowfully. “If even your talents could not accomplish it, how could I entertain such thoughts again?”

Unchi comforted Yang with all manner of words and teased him for a long while before saying, “Though I have failed to bring Lady Jeong, I have brought a woman ten times more beautiful than she.”

“I have seen many beautiful women, but none like Lady Jeong. Please do not jest, my friend.”

“How could I make sport of a sick man? I have left her in the outer quarters. She is truly beautiful enough to bring an entire city to ruin. Go and see for yourself.”

Half believing and half doubting, Yang rose with difficulty and went to the outer quarters. Sure enough, a woman dressed in white mourning clothes was sitting there. Her rounded face was like the bright moon in the autumn sky, and her clear eyes were like the stars at dawn. Her graceful beauty was beyond compare. At a single glance, Yang saw that she was Lady Jeong, for whom he had longed both asleep and awake. His senses were overwhelmed, as though he were drunk or had lost his mind. He could scarcely contain his delight and happiness. Thereafter, Yang’s illness gradually abated.

Meanwhile, wishing to meet Seo Hwadam, who lived in Hoju, Unchi prepared gifts and set out on his journey. At that time, Seo Hwadam instructed his attendant boy, “At around midday today, a scholar surnamed Jeon will come to visit. Sweep the thatched cottage clean.”

Unchi reached the mountain gate and walked slowly, admiring his surroundings. Green pines and bamboo grew thickly, and water flowed gently through the valley. Deer wandered about in search of their companions, and white cranes danced and played. It was truly another realm, unlike the human world. When Unchi reached a gate woven from branches among the bamboo and knocked upon it, a boy came out and asked, “Are you not Master Jeon?”

“How do you know me?”

“My master told me this morning.”

Greatly pleased, Unchi had the boy present his gifts and requested an audience with Hwadam. Hwadam immediately welcomed Unchi into the thatched cottage. Having observed the proper courtesies between guest and host, they began to converse. Unchi said, “I have heard your great reputation resounding like thunder and have travelled a thousand ri without considering the distance too great. I beg you to instruct me, Master.”

Hwadam replied modestly, “Master Jeon, you have come to put me to the test. What knowledge of the Way could I possibly possess to deserve such extravagant praise? I have heard that your magical arts are so accomplished that there is nothing you cannot do, and I have long wished to meet you. Now that we have met, I count it among the good fortunes of my life.”

Unchi rose and expressed his gratitude, and the two conversed throughout the day. Hwadam urged a maidservant to prepare wine and food. He then drew a sword and thrust it into the wall. The immortals’ Yeolchulju wine flowed into a wine jar, and in a short while the jar was filled. Hwadam immediately withdrew the sword. Upon the northern wall hung a scroll depicting a splendid pavilion. A window in the painting opened, and a celestial maiden dressed in colourful garments came out carrying a table laden with wine and food. She placed the table before Unchi, raised a cup, and offered him wine. Unchi accepted it and found the wine exceedingly fragrant. Expressing his gratitude to Hwadam, he said, “I have reached the realm of the immortals and been permitted to taste wine as clear as jade and rare delicacies. I am exceedingly grateful.”

Hwadam smiled and said, “Why do you praise this unremarkable wine so highly?”

While the two were conversing, a scholar suddenly entered, wearing a hood woven from kudzu fibres and simple clothes. “Who is our guest?” he asked.

Hwadam replied, “This is Master Jeon of Namseobu.” Turning to Unchi, he said, “This is my younger brother, Yongdam. As he has never met you before, he has failed to observe the proper courtesies towards a guest. Please forgive him.”

Unchi raised his eyes and examined Yongdam. His brows and eyes were clear, his frame was imposing, and his majestic bearing was enough to astonish anyone. Presently, Yongdam paid his respects to Unchi and said, “I have long heard of your accomplished magical arts, Master, and it is only today that I have had the opportunity to meet you. It is rather late indeed. I would very much like to see your magical arts.”

“What magical arts could an unworthy man such as myself possess?”

Yongdam earnestly entreated him two or three times. Wishing to put him to the test, Unchi immediately recited an incantation. The cap Yongdam was wearing turned into the head of an ox with horns three bal in length. The ox’s head fell upon a stone, its eyes twitching and its mouth opening. Seeing that his cap had turned into an ox’s head, Yongdam became angry and immediately recited an incantation. This time, the gat Unchi was wearing turned into a pig’s head. The pig’s head fell upon a rock, bared its tusks, moved its ears, and crawled about. Unchi thought to himself, ‘This man’s talents are extraordinary. He is well worth contending with.’

Unchi recited an incantation towards the pig’s head. It turned into a long, three-pronged spear. Yongdam likewise recited an incantation towards the ox’s head, which turned into a great sword. The long spear and the great sword rose into the air and fought each other, their blades gleaming brilliantly in the sunlight.

Yongdam then threw his fan and recited an incantation. The sword and the fan turned into a red dragon and an azure dragon. Unchi likewise threw the fan ornament he was holding in his hand. The long spear and the fan ornament turned into a white dragon and a black dragon.

The four dragons fought one another. Clouds and mist grew thick, and thunder and lightning resounded. Neither side could prevail at first, but gradually the strength of the azure dragon and the red dragon began to diminish. Hwadam thought to himself, ‘If these two continue contending with their magical arts in this manner, something unfortunate will surely happen.’

Hwadam picked up the water dropper used for pouring water into an inkstone and threw it. Suddenly, everything fell to the ground and returned to its original form. Unchi first picked up his gat and put it on, then retrieved his fan ornament. He addressed Yongdam in gentle words, but Yongdam would not retrieve his fan or his cap. Taking his leave, Unchi said, “Today, I have presumed to engage in a contest of magical arts and offended you, Master. My offence is grave. I shall visit you again another day to apologise.” Having said this, Unchi departed.

After seeing Unchi off, Hwadam reprimanded Yongdam. “You brought forth an azure dragon and a red dragon, while Unchi brought forth a white dragon and a black dragon. Azure corresponds to wood, red to fire, white to metal, and black to water. According to the principles of the Five Phases, metal overcomes wood, and water overcomes fire. How could you possibly defeat Unchi? Moreover, why would you engage in a needless contest of magical arts with a guest who has come to my house and attempt to harm him?” Yongdam apologised. Inwardly, however, he was exceedingly angry with Unchi and harboured intentions of harming him.

10. The Road to Mount Yeongju

Three days later, Unchi visited Hwadam again. Hwadam said, “There is something I wish to ask of you. Would you be willing to undertake it?”

“What is it?”

“In the midst of the Southern Sea lies a great mountain called Mount Hwa. Upon that mountain lives a master of the Way, known by his Daoist name, Master Unsu. I once studied under him. He has sent me letters on several occasions, but I have yet to reply. Now that I have met you, would you be able to make the journey on my behalf?”

Unchi readily agreed. Hwadam said, “It seems to me that, as Mount Hwa lies in the midst of the sea, the journey will not be an easy one.”

“Though I possess little talent, I shall return in the blink of an eye.”

Hwadam still appeared doubtful. Unchi wondered inwardly whether Hwadam was looking down upon him and said, “If I fail to return in the blink of an eye, I shall never again pass beyond this mountain gate, even if it means dying here.”

“If you truly mean what you say, then go. I am concerned that you might make some mistake.”

Hwadam immediately wrote a letter and handed it to Unchi. Taking the letter, Unchi transformed himself into a young Haedongcheong falcon. He rose into the air and flew towards the midst of the sea.

Looking ahead, however, he saw an enormous net that had appeared from nowhere, blocking his way. When Unchi attempted to fly over it, the net rose as high as he did and continued to obstruct his path. Unchi flew higher still, trying by every means to pass over the net. The net continued to rise after him until it reached the very limits of the sky, while the cords along its lower edge lay submerged in the sea. Unchi attempted to fly high around it to the left and right. Yet on either side, the net stretched to the very limits of the sky, and there was no way for him to reach Mount Hwa. For more than ten days, Unchi exerted himself in every possible way, prepared even to die in the attempt, but in the end he could do nothing.

At last, he returned to Hwadam and told him of the hardships he had endured at sea. Hwadam said, “You spoke so boldly. Now, what would you say to passing beyond the mountain gate?”

Ashamed, Unchi attempted to flee. Hwadam perceived his intentions, transformed himself into a wildcat, and sprang at him. In haste, Unchi transformed himself into a young falcon and attempted to fly away. Hwadam then transformed himself into a blue lion, seized Unchi in his jaws, threw him to the ground, and reprimanded him loudly. “A fellow such as you practises sorcery, deceives the king, treats his superiors with contempt, and recklessly makes mischief. How could I not kill you!”

Unchi pleaded, “Not knowing that your magical arts were so accomplished, Master, I have offended a man of great distinction. I deserve to die ten thousand deaths. I have an elderly mother. I beg you, Master, to spare my life.”

“I shall spare you this time. Never again behave so recklessly, and devote yourself to caring for your mother. After your mother passes away, what would you say to accompanying me to Mount Yeongju and cultivating the Way of the immortals?”

“I shall do as you instruct me, Master.”

Unchi took his leave of Hwadam and returned home. Thereafter, he refrained from practising sorcery and devoted himself to supporting his mother.

Time flowed like water. When Unchi’s mother passed away, he observed the prescribed rites, buried her in the ancestral burial grounds, and entered the three years of mourning.

One day, Hwadam came to visit. Unchi hurried out to welcome him, observed the proper courtesies, and took his seat. Hwadam said, “There is a promise between us, and although I know that you are still in mourning, I have come to see you. What would you say to capturing the nine-tailed fox that dwells upon that mountain, confining it in a stone chest, and setting fire to its cave?”

“If you would now rid us of that fox, Master, it would truly be a great fortune for the entire kingdom.”

Hwadam said, “I have come to take you with me. Make ready your belongings.”

Unchi was exceedingly pleased. He divided his household property among his servants and said, “I now intend to bid you farewell for ever. Live in peace, and see that you continue to uphold the ancestral rites of my family.”

Unchi visited the graves of his ancestors and paid his final respects. He then accompanied Hwadam, riding upon clouds towards Mount Yeongju.

What happened thereafter is unknown.
