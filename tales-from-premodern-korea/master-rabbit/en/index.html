Tales from Premodern Korea

The Tale of Master Rabbit

The Tale of Master Rabbit is a work of premodern Korean fiction whose author and date of composition are unknown. Around seventy versions of the work have survived, with considerable differences not only in the structure and content of the story but also in its ending. Many of the versions familiar to readers today are adaptations for children, and some versions, including the pansori text by Shin Jae-hyo, have happy endings.

The edition presented by Jin-in-Seoul is based on Tosaeng-jeon, a Gyeongpan edition published in 1848. This version satirises not only the folly of those in power and the self-deception of officials seeking advancement, but also the rabbit’s fanciful dreams of sudden wealth and a comfortable life. Jin-in-Seoul has chosen this edition for these literary qualities. The work is presented under its widely used modern Korean title, Tokki-jeon, rendered in English as The Tale of Master Rabbit. The chapter divisions and titles have been newly established.

1. The Sea King’s Illness

During the Chenghua reign of the Ming dynasty, Ongang, King Gwanghyeok, ascended the throne of the Palace of the Northern Sea. One day, he happened to fall ill, and his condition grew increasingly serious. No medicine proved effective, and the entire Sea Palace was thrown into turmoil.

One day, a Daoist master suddenly appeared and said, “Your Majesty’s illness cannot be cured even by the medicine used by the immortals of the Three Sacred Mountains. If you send someone into the human world to catch a rabbit, take out its liver and make it into a medicinal pill for you to swallow, you will soon recover.”

The Sea King assembled his officials to discuss what the Daoist master had said. One official stepped forward from the ranks and addressed the king. “Though I possess little talent, I shall go into the human world and bring back a rabbit alive.”

Everyone turned to look. It was Byeoljubu, a cousin of the turtle, though they bore different surnames. The Sea King was greatly pleased and said, “Your loyalty is truly admirable!”

He immediately summoned a painter, ordered him to paint a likeness of a rabbit, and gave it to Byeoljubu. As Byeoljubu took the picture and prepared to take his leave, the king cautioned him. “In sending you into the human world, what concerns me most is the fishermen’s nets and hooks. When I was young and travelling about to see the sights, I was caught on a fisherman’s hook by the waters of Seonghwasu and very nearly died. I managed to escape only because the fishing line broke as I shook my body. You must be careful and bring back a rabbit.” He then bestowed wine upon Byeoljubu.

Byeoljubu took leave of the king and went home to bid farewell to his wife and children. In an instant, he crossed the vast sea and reached the human world. Delighted to have arrived safely, he followed the shore in search of mountains deep inland.

2. The Meeting of the Rabbit and Byeoljubu

It was the third month of spring, when the flowers were in bloom. Not knowing which way to go, Byeoljubu wandered among the mountains and rivers on either side, examining everything carefully.

The mountains were not high, yet an air of beauty surrounded them, and streams flowed gently through places thick with trees and grass. Cliffs rose steeply, and birds and beasts cried mournfully. Peacocks and phoenixes wandered among the profusion of extraordinary flowers and lovely grasses, while bees and butterflies played where the air was filled with the fragrance of blossoms. Orioles flew among the green willows. It was truly a place of scenic beauty in the human world.

As Byeoljubu made his way up a mountain, enjoying the scenery, a creature suddenly came strutting down from the mountainside, grazing on grass and playing among the flowers. Byeoljubu concealed himself and took out the picture of the rabbit. Sure enough, it was a rabbit. Delighted, Byeoljubu thought to himself, ‘If I catch that rabbit and present it to our great king, and His Majesty recovers, I shall surely become a meritorious subject of the first rank.’

Byeoljubu stretched out his long neck, approached the rabbit and greeted him courteously. “My respects to you, Master Rabbit.”

The rabbit looked at the softshell turtle and laughed. “How is it that you know my name and address me by it? Are you the son of a pond turtle? What a long neck you have!”

The softshell turtle sat beside the rabbit, remarked that they had never met before, and exchanged names with him. He then asked, “In what year were you born, Master Rabbit? And what pleasures do you find in wandering among these green mountains and clear streams?”

The rabbit smiled and replied, “I have travelled throughout the world for more than three hundred years. In the depths of the mountains, surrounded by ridge upon ridge, flowers of every kind are in full bloom, auspicious clouds hang softly in the air, green pines spread their branches, and clear waters flow gently by. I wander without a care through places filled with fragrance, drink my fill of the dew that gathers on every kind of grass, and savour the scents of the woods, flowers and grasses to my heart’s content.

“I come and go through deserted mountains that belong to no one, without a quarrel or dispute, eating wild fruits as I please. From time to time, I wander among countless peaks and valleys, and when I look down upon the whole world, my heart feels refreshed. Such pleasures are difficult to put into words. If you too wish to taste the pleasures of this world, why not come along with me?”

The softshell turtle replied, “It is a pleasure to hear you speak so highly of the scenery of the human world. I do not actually live in the human world. I am a softshell turtle in the service of the King of the Northern Sea, holding the official post of Jubu.

“While I was on night duty at the Sea Palace, the King of the Eastern Sea sent an envoy to invite our king to his birthday banquet. Our great king happened to fall ill with a complaint that prevented him from passing urine, and as he was unwell, he could not attend.

“The Crown Prince ordered me to go into the human world, travel along the shore and through various other places, and find out where the fishermen were casting their lines. Having come into this world and made my inquiries, I was on my way back when I saw the flowers and grasses flourishing here. I stopped for a little while to admire them and had the pleasure of meeting you. I cannot tell you how delighted I am.

“Since you speak so highly of the scenery of the human world, allow me to boast a little about the beautiful scenery of our Sea Palace. Please listen carefully.”

3. The Temptation of the Sea Palace

“The buildings of the Sea Palace are constructed with foundation stones of precious amber, pillars of coral, beams of yellow amber and roof tiles of blue jade. Crystal blinds hang from them, balustrades of white jade are adorned with pure gold, and mountains are fashioned from five-coloured clouds for our enjoyment of the beautiful scenery.

“We enjoy music of every kind by day and night, while palace maidens adorned with seven precious treasures serve us Thousand-Day Wine in glass cups resting on amber stands. Imagine what a pleasure that must be.

“In the morning we ride the mist, and in the evening we ride the clouds, travelling throughout the world in an instant. We play jade flutes held sideways to our lips and roam freely through the air. How could one possibly describe such serene pleasures?

“Yet you boast of the insignificant scenery of this noisy world. How shortsighted of you, Master Rabbit. Suppose winds and clouds covered the land on every side, rain came pouring down as though water were being thrown from great wooden bowls, and thunder and lightning shook the heavens. If you took shelter in a crevice between the rocks and the mountain collapsed, your little body would be crushed to powder.”

Startled by these words, the rabbit said, “Never speak of such dreadful things again.”

The softshell turtle continued, “In the bitter cold of winter, when white snow covers heaven and earth and not even a burrow can be seen, how will you provide for your wife and children, sheltering as best you can in a crevice between the rocks? How will you yourself escape hunger and thirst?

“When the three months of winter have passed and the breath of spring returns to the shaded valleys, you will rise from your cold hole among the rocks and hurry up the mountain in search of a cool place. How will you feel when a hunter’s arrows and bullets fly over your head? How will you feel when a falconer roams about with his hunting dogs?

“If you come down to the plains, herdboys with newly made sickles slung over their shoulders will surround you, shouting. With that almost nonexistent tail tucked between your legs, your little eyes wide open and your short feet moving as fast as they can, you will rush about wildly, stumbling and tumbling as you flee. With your heart ablaze and your wits deserting you, when will you find time to admire the flowers and grasses, and with what nose will you breathe in their fragrance?

“If you think carefully and follow me into the Sea Palace, you will behold the scenery of the immortals, eat heavenly peaches, spend your days drunk on Thousand-Day Wine and spend the rest of your life in the company of beautiful women. You will enjoy both wealth and high rank. Think it over carefully.”

The rabbit listened attentively for a long while before speaking. “Your words, Jubu, have truly brightened my heart. I have had an unfortunate life myself. I lost my wife in middle age and then lost my only son. Unable to live alone, I married again in the twelfth month of last year. My wife is a beauty without equal, and our love for each other fills a small gourd bowl to the brim, a large wooden bowl to overflowing, and a dalbak bowl fuller and fuller still. We cannot bear to be apart for even a moment.

“If I were to go straight from here to the Sea Palace, my family would be sick with worry, not knowing where I had gone. I shall go home for a moment and tell them. Please sit here and wait for me, Jubu.”

The softshell turtle was secretly delighted and thought to himself, ‘If this fellow goes home, his family will surely try to stop him. Now that I have got hold of him, I shall take him with me.’ Then he said,

“As a man of courage, why should you be so bound to a woman that, like the son of a government office attendant, you must ask permission even for such a thing?”

The rabbit was uneasy at these words, but the remark about the son of a government office attendant angered him. “Very well, I shall go without telling them. How long will it take me to return, and how are we to travel across the water?”

The softshell turtle was overjoyed. “If you are willing to come, you need not worry about the water.”

The rabbit went down to the water’s edge with the softshell turtle, climbed onto his back and closed his eyes. The softshell turtle floated upon the water, crossed the vast sea in an instant and arrived at the gates of the Sea Palace. When the rabbit opened his eyes, he saw a three-storey pavilion rising amid clouds of many colours. Above it hung a sign bearing the words ‘Palace of the Northern Sea’, and soldiers stood in ranks guarding the entrance.

4. The Rabbit’s Liver

The softshell turtle said to the rabbit, “Wait here. I shall be back shortly.”

The softshell turtle entered the Sea Palace, presented himself before the king and reported how he had tricked the rabbit into coming with him. The king was greatly pleased. He immediately seated himself upon his throne, arranged the Crown Prince, the royal family and the civil and military officials in ranks on either side, and ordered his guards to seize the rabbit at once. Guards with the heads of fish and the faces of ghosts rushed out together and seized him. The rabbit wet himself as he was dragged inside, taking tiny, hurried steps. Looking to either side, he saw soldiers guarding the upper part of the palace and guards standing in ranks below. The spectacle was imposing and solemn.

The king said to the rabbit, “I have a grave illness deep within my belly, and no medicine has proved effective. Unexpectedly, a Daoist master told me that eating your liver would do me good. That is why I have had you brought here. You are a little beast, and I am the great king of the Sea Palace. What do you say to taking the liver from your belly to cure the illness deep in my very marrow?”

The king ordered the rabbit to be bound, and the guards on either side rushed forward and tied him up. The rabbit was terrified and did not know what to do. He thought quietly to himself, ‘How could I have known that the softshell turtle would deceive me and lead me to my death?’

The rabbit lamented bitterly. “Had I known that this would happen, why would I ever have come here, however magnificent the Sea Palace might be? I was perfectly comfortable where I was. Even if they made me an outer robe of ginseng, an official’s cap of heavenly peaches and a walking stick of crystal, what son of a bitch would so much as look at the Sea Palace!

“I have left my home and travelled ten million ri across the water, only to meet my death. How miserable and infuriating! My family will have no idea what has become of me!”

The rabbit sat for a long while, then suddenly thought of a trick. He looked up at the heavens and laughed aloud. The king asked, “Why are you laughing?”

Without the slightest change in his expression, the rabbit replied, “I am laughing for no other reason than that the softshell turtle’s conduct amuses me.”

“What do you mean?”

The rabbit laughed again and said, “If the softshell turtle receives an official stipend from the kingdom and serves his sovereign, he ought to devote all his loyalty and strength to that service. Had he told me of Your Majesty’s illness when he met me beside the clear stream, I would not have begrudged you my little liver. Yet he said nothing whatsoever about it and did nothing but boast of the Sea Palace.

“Not only have I wished all my life to see the Sea Palace, but the people of the world have grown so vicious that I came here partly to seek refuge. How could I have known that things would turn out like this?

“Instead of fulfilling his proper duties as a subject, he merely babbled like a suckling infant. Is that not foolish and ignorant? It is rather like sending someone out to buy Cheongsimhwan when a patient is already suffering from a sudden and severe illness.”

The king was furious. “How deceitful your words are! I demand your liver at once, and here you are talking about something else!”

The king’s command was as cutting as frost. The rabbit did not know what to do and kept letting out one fart after another, yet his expression remained as composed as ever. With a half-smile, he addressed the king. “Whenever people in the human world meet me, they ask for my liver to use as medicine. Unable to fend them all off, I took out my liver and hid it in a secluded place before going about my affairs. I happened to meet Byeoljubu and came here without it, never imagining that this would happen.”

He then turned to the softshell turtle and scolded him. “You stupid creature! Now that I see His Majesty, it is clear that his illness is extremely serious. Why did you not tell me about this?”

The king grew even angrier. “The liver is attached to the five internal organs. How can you take it out and put it back whenever you please? You persist in treating me with contempt!” He ordered those around him to cut open the rabbit’s belly and take out his liver.

The rabbit addressed the king. “If you cut open my belly now and find no liver, whom will you ask for one? The dead cannot be brought back to life, and no amount of regret will do you any good. If Your Majesty spares my life, I shall bring you my liver.”

The king grew more furious still and urged his attendants to hurry. As a warrior rushed forward with a sword to cut open his belly, the rabbit still showed no change in his expression and hastily addressed the king. “There is clear evidence that I take out my liver and leave it behind. Please examine it.”

“What evidence is there?”

“There are three openings between my legs. Through one I pass my stools, through another I pass my urine, and through the remaining one I take out my liver. Please see for yourself.”

The king found this extraordinary and ordered his attendants to turn the rabbit upside down and examine his groin. Sure enough, it was as he had said. The king clapped his hands and laughed aloud. “In that case, through which opening do you put your liver back, and why is your liver said to have medicinal properties?”

Only then did the rabbit regain his composure and reply, “When I put my liver back, I swallow it through my mouth. Unlike other beasts, I absorb all the vital essences of spring, summer, autumn and winter, of yin and yang and the Five Phases, and of the sun, moon and stars. I feed upon the morning dew, the evening mist and the frost of dawn, so that pure energies gather within my internal organs. That is why my liver is said to have medicinal properties.”

The king thought this sounded plausible and consulted his officials. They addressed him. “Everything this fellow says is deceitful. Please have his belly cut open.”

The king agreed with them. “Your words sound plausible enough, but you may have put your liver back and forgotten about it. Cutting open your belly would be the surest way to find out.” He then bestowed upon the rabbit the title of Duke Rabbit and raised him to the rank of the Three High Councillors.

The rabbit said, “For a little creature of the mountains to receive such generous treatment from Your Majesty, and even to be granted a title of nobility, leaves me deeply humbled and moved beyond words. I beg permission to return to the human world with the softshell turtle and bring back my liver.”

The king was greatly pleased and held a grand banquet in the rabbit’s honour. At this point, the torrent catfish, who held the post of Chief Royal Remonstrator, addressed the king. “The rabbit’s words cannot be trusted. It would be proper to keep the rabbit here in the Sea Palace and send only the softshell turtle to fetch his liver.”

The rabbit secretly wished he could shoot the torrent catfish with a noiseless matchlock gun. The king was furious. “The matter has already been settled. What nonsense are you talking!” He ordered the torrent catfish to be imprisoned by the Royal Tribunal.

The rabbit spent the whole day drinking, becoming exceedingly drunk and enjoying himself. Then he said, “Judging by Your Majesty’s condition, you are likely to die very soon. Even if King Yama were your uncle, even if you wore an outer robe made of the herb of immortality and an official’s cap made of ox bezoar, your life would still be in danger. I shall go and fetch my liver at once.”

The king summoned the softshell turtle and instructed him. “The rabbit’s words are reasonable. Rather than killing him to no purpose, it would be better for you to go together and fetch his liver. Carry him out on your back and bring back the liver quickly.” He also issued official orders that food and fodder be provided wherever they passed.

5. Return to Land

As the rabbit rode upon the softshell turtle’s back towards the world above the water once more, he thought to himself, ‘I was foolish enough to be deceived by you in the first place and nearly lost my life, and that Sea King is a matchless fool himself. Now my chance to live has come. Where in the world is there a man or beast who can take out his liver and put it back again? Somehow, I must keep the softshell turtle in good humour and get out of here quickly.’

The rabbit said to the softshell turtle, “If you had told me of the king’s illness beforehand and we had brought my liver with us, we would not have had to make this needless journey, and the king would have recovered at once. You have been such a fool that you have put yourself to all this trouble on my account. When I bring back my liver and receive my reward, I shall share it with you.”

Hearing these words, the softshell turtle became convinced that the rabbit’s liver really was outside his body. “It is true that I tricked you into coming with me for the sake of the king’s illness. How could I have imagined that you could take out your liver and put it back again? If that is indeed the case, it will be good for us both. Why should I begrudge myself a little more trouble?”

The softshell turtle set the rabbit down at the water’s edge. Thinking himself safe at last, the rabbit ran towards his burrow, stumbling and tumbling as he went, only to become caught in a net with no apparent means of escape. The rabbit scolded a fly. “You contemptible little creature! I shall wipe out your entire breed!”

Indignant at the rabbit’s threat to wipe out his breed, the fly summoned his fellows and said, “The rabbit is caught in a net and is about to die, yet he still threatens and insults me. A fellow like this should not be allowed to die in peace. Let us all gather around him, feed upon him and lay our eggs at the tips of every hair on his body.”

The flies gathered at once, feeding upon the rabbit and laying their eggs. The rabbit endured the torment, yet, fearing that the flies might not lay enough eggs, he rolled about and continued to abuse them. The flies grew even more furious and laid their eggs over the rabbit’s entire body, leaving not a single patch uncovered. When the man who had set the net arrived, the rabbit pretended to be dead. Seeing the eggs the flies had laid, the man declared that the rabbit was rotten and threw him away.

Only then did the rabbit return home and meet his wife. The female rabbit was horrified to see her husband’s body covered in fly eggs. “How did you come to be in such a state? How could I ever have imagined that you would return alive?”

The male rabbit told her everything that had happened from beginning to end. Upon hearing his account, the female rabbit went to where the softshell turtle was and abused him. “You horrible, dreadful creature! What grudge could you possibly have borne against us in a previous life, that you should lure away the husband with whom I was to grow old and try to take out his liver? Had my husband not been so clever, would he not have lost his life?

“With such wickedness in your heart, may that long neck of yours snap as you go on your way, or may your head burst open and kill you! Far from recovering by eating a liver, your Sea King will grow even sicker and will not die in peace!”

Unable to contain his indignation, the softshell turtle said, “You bitch! Hold your tongue and listen to me. However fierce a woman may be, how can she behave so viciously? What a spiteful and insolent creature you are!”

The male rabbit came down and said to the softshell turtle, “You have gone to a great deal of trouble carrying me back and forth across the vast sea. I have nothing to give you as a token of appreciation. You poor fellow.”

The softshell turtle said, “You have done nothing but disgrace our Sea Palace, and now you expect me to return empty-handed without even giving me the liver!”

The rabbit looked up at the heavens and laughed aloud. “However stupid you may be, must you go to such lengths over your failure to obtain my liver? If my relatives and old friends find out about this, they will surely break your back in two. Be off with you.” Then the rabbit and his wife, carrying their daughter piggyback, scurried into the bushes and vanished.

6. The Deaths of Byeoljubu and the Sea King

Finding himself utterly helpless, the softshell turtle lamented, “I have been deceived by that cunning rabbit. How can I return and face the king? I would be better off dead.” He composed a written message, attached it to a rock, and struck his head against the rock, thud after thud, until he died.

Meanwhile, the king found it strange that there had been no word from the softshell turtle since his departure. He sent the turtle, Byeoljubu’s older cousin and the Director of the Royal Academy, to find out what had happened. The turtle immediately went to the water’s edge and looked around. There was a written message attached to a rock, and beside it lay the body of the softshell turtle. Overcome with grief, the turtle wept bitterly, gathered up the body and the message, and returned to report to the king. Taking pity on him, the king provided silk and ordered that he be laid to rest.

At this time, the Superintendent of the Royal Pharmacy, the octopus; the Chief Royal Remonstrator, the torrent catfish; the Oesipyeong, the crucian carp; the Oesangseong, the skate; the Royal Secretary, the abalone; and the Royal Scholar, the sweetfish, submitted a memorial to the king. “Not only has that little rabbit from the mountains deceived our sovereign and his subjects, but he has subjected us to countless humiliations. Please issue a special command to the mountain deity to seize the rabbit without delay and put him to death by severe punishment.”

The Chief State Councillor, the whale; the Left State Councillor, the mullet; and the Right State Councillor, the croaker, addressed the king. “It seems unlikely that the mountain deity will be able to catch the rabbit. It would be better to send the finest soldiers of the Sea Palace to surround the mountain where the rabbit lives and capture him, or to bring down a great rain, submerge the mountain and exterminate the rabbit’s entire clan without leaving a single descendant.”

The king said, “Your words are not right. Emperor Gaozu of the Han dynasty was a ruler of the human world, yet when he fell ill, he declared that his life was in the hands of Heaven and dismissed his physician.

“How much more foolish of me, who am called a deity, to have heedlessly listened to the words of a Daoist master, committed such deeds and been humiliated by a rabbit.

“If, unable to restrain a little anger, we embark upon another undertaking, and our resentment against one individual brings calamity upon others as a fire spreads through a forest, we shall merely add another wrongdoing to the one we have already committed.

“How foolish it would be of me, ignorant of the will of Heaven, to harbour resentment against a little rabbit. Speak no more of this matter.”

Having finished speaking, the king sighed deeply. He then summoned the Crown Prince and the Left and Right State Councillors to the chamber where he lay, entrusted them with his last wishes, and soon passed away. At the time of his death, the king was eighteen hundred years old and had occupied the throne for twelve hundred years.

The Crown Prince, accompanied by the civil and military officials, announced the king’s death and began the mourning rites. All the creatures of the sea wept bitterly as though they had lost their own parents. The sound of their weeping was like that of boiling water.

Five days later, upon completion of the ceremony of putting on formal mourning clothes, the Crown Prince ascended the throne. He proclaimed a general pardon and bestowed titles of nobility. Dispensing with the celebratory ceremony of crying out ‘A thousand years!’, he sent envoys to the Sea Palaces of the east, west, south and north to announce the death. Chugyung, King Gwangni of the Southern Sea, Amyeong, King Gwangyeon of the Eastern Sea, and Geoseung, King Gwangdeok of the Western Sea, all came in person to offer their condolences. It was a magnificent sight.

A Note on This Novel

Rabbits are cunning, clever, swift and nimble. In the old tales of both East and West, they appear sometimes as good characters and sometimes as foolish ones. The most famous rabbit in literature is probably the White Rabbit who takes a watch out of his pocket in Lewis Carroll’s Alice’s Adventures in Wonderland. In the opening chapter of Kenneth Grahame’s The Wind in the Willows, rabbits appear as annoying creatures trying to extort a toll from Mole. And reading Richard Adams’s Watership Down, one discovers how varied the personalities within a community of rabbits can be. They are sometimes brave and sometimes foolish, they have myths and legends of their own, and there are even rabbits endowed with a mysterious gift of foresight.

In the West, a rabbit’s foot is sometimes regarded as a charm that brings good luck. I first learnt about rabbit’s feet through Mission: Impossible III, starring Tom Cruise. Curious, I looked into it and was astonished to discover that people had used not only charms fashioned in the shape of a rabbit’s foot, but actual rabbits’ feet as well.

There is no particular reason why I chose The Tale of Master Rabbit as the second work in Jin-in-Seoul’s Tales from Premodern Korea. It is simply a work of premodern Korean fiction with lively characters and an entertaining story. A rabbit as swift as Achilles, as cunning as Odysseus and as enamoured of himself as Narcissus; Byeoljubu, who professes loyalty while secretly dreaming of advancement and power; and a Sea King willing to sacrifice an innocent life to cure his own illness. How different their world is from ours in the twenty-first century.
