## 2. Translation Approach

- Use the established modern Korean edition as the source for foreign-language translations.
- Do not adapt the work into a children's story or didactic fable.
- Preserve the source's humour, insults, satire, exaggeration and tragic ending.
- Maintain both the biological characteristics of animals and their human social relationships.
- Translate into natural modern English without artificial archaism.
- Make the text readable without separate annotations or footnotes.
- Preserve the final Korean edition's paragraph structure one-to-one.
- Do not combine events, characters, settings or endings from other editions.
- Do not embellish or soften characters' behaviour or values according to the translator's judgement.

## 8. Character Relationships and Dialogue

- Preserve the hierarchy between sovereign and subject.
- Convey the changes between deference, familiarity, contempt and mockery as Byeoljubu tempts the rabbit.
- Preserve the rabbit's wit and brazen manner of speaking.
- Retain the affection and humorous dialogue between the married rabbits.
- Do not omit or soften insults and curses.
- Do not change kinship relationships or forms of address to conform to ordinary English-speaking family relationships.
- Render the kinship form of address 형 as Hyung. Distinguish blood relationships from forms of address; do not replace the form of address itself with brother or cousin.
- Convey Korean honorific expressions not found in English through vocabulary and tone.

## 9. Literary Characteristics

Convey the source's satire and humour through its own expressions and situations, without adding new jokes.

Preserve Byeoljubu's loyalty and ambition, the rabbit's will to survive and deception, and the Sea King's authority and final self-reflection.

Retain the source ending in which the Sea King and Byeoljubu die. Do not add the immortal's rescue or the Sea King's recovery found in other editions.
