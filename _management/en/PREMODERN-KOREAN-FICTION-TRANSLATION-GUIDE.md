# Translation and Editing Principles for Premodern Korean Fiction

In this document, premodern Korean fiction refers to Korean fiction written before the modern era. The term denotes a historical period rather than a judgment of literary merit or canonical status.

**1. Source Text**

A source edition is established for each work, with its title, date of publication, and source identified. Material from different editions is not arbitrarily combined.

**2. Original Names and Titles**

The titles of works and the names of characters follow the source text rather than their more widely known forms. This principle applies to both modern Korean and foreign-language translations.

**3. Preservation of Content**

Events, characters, dialogue, actions, descriptions, and figurative expressions are not arbitrarily omitted or abridged. Nothing absent from the source text is added.

**4. Historical Values**

Characters’ actions and events are not embellished or softened to conform to contemporary ethical, political, or social values. The values, human relationships, social structures, and religious worldviews of the source text are preserved.

**5. Modern Korean**

Vocabulary and sentence structures accessible to modern readers are used while preserving the style, atmosphere, and voices of the original. Accurate transmission of meaning takes precedence over the retention of archaic linguistic forms.

**6. Reading Without Annotations**

Difficult archaic expressions, official titles, and religious terms are rendered in accessible language so that the text can be read without separate annotations or footnotes. Terms intelligible from context are retained, with their meanings conveyed naturally upon first appearance where necessary. Historical official titles and distinctive names must not disappear through paraphrase; the name is retained and its meaning conveyed naturally at first appearance when needed.

**7. Chapters and Paragraphs**

The text may be divided into chapters and given chapter titles according to the development of events and the narrative flow. Paragraphs are arranged according to the continuity of dialogue, actions, and descriptions. The sequence of events and the narrative structure remain unchanged. Once the modern Korean edition is finalized, foreign-language editions preserve its paragraph boundaries one-to-one, without splitting or merging paragraphs to follow target-language conventions.

**8. Dialogue Attribution and Repetition**

Repetitive expressions such as “said,” “asked,” and “replied” may be reduced when the speaker is clear. Expressions necessary to identify speakers or convey their emotions and actions are retained. Repetition that serves a literary purpose is preserved.

**9. No Adaptation**

Works are not adapted for specific readerships, including children, young adults, or students. The content of the source text is not altered for moral, educational, or commercial purposes.

**10. Foreign-Language Translation**

Every translation into another language is made directly from the finalized modern Korean edition. Events, characters, dialogue, descriptions, figurative expressions, paragraph structure, and narrative rhythm are preserved. Korean historical and cultural elements are not replaced with those of the target culture, and the rendering of personal names, place names, official titles, and objects remains consistent throughout each work. If an error in the modern Korean edition is discovered during translation, the source text is consulted, the Korean edition is corrected first, and the correction is incorporated into all foreign-language editions.

**11. Inconsistencies and Ambiguities**

Inconsistencies in ages, chronology, or events within the source text are not arbitrarily corrected. Ambiguous expressions are not clarified through conjecture. Inconsistencies inherent in the source text are distinguished from errors introduced during translation.

**12. Interpretation and Verification**

Difficult archaic expressions and damaged passages are examined using dictionaries, relevant documents, and their surrounding context. Other editions and existing translations may be consulted as references for interpretation. Unverified content is not supplied through conjecture.

**13. Review and Revision**

Revision is undertaken to correct mistranslations, omissions, distortions of meaning, and defects in expression. Natural sentences that faithfully convey the original meaning are not repeatedly revised according to the personal preferences of translators or editors. The same editorial principles apply to the modern Korean and foreign-language editions. Proposed changes are reviewed and approved before application, and the revised text is compared again with the established source.

# Shared Practical Translation Standards and English Glossary

## I. Shared Translation Standards

**1. Source text**  
Translate directly from the established modern Korean edition. Consult the source edition for interpretation or error verification. If the Korean edition needs correction, present the proposed correction for review and obtain approval before applying it. Do not repeatedly revise established translations according to personal preference.

**2. Style**  
Use modern English while retaining the character of a tale from premodern Korea. Avoid artificial archaism, contemporary American colloquialism and Western fantasy adaptation. Establish the narrator's voice and sentence rhythm separately for each work.

**3. Events and magical arts**  
The stranger the event, the more matter-of-fact the narration. Do not add light, sound, movement, emotion, suspense or explanations absent from the source. Preserve the order in which events unfold and information reaches the reader.

**4. Dialogue and hierarchy**  
Reflect relations between sovereign and subject, parent and child, teacher and disciple, and men and women in tone and sentence structure. Do not translate every form of address mechanically. Do not add dialogue tags where the speaker is clear; preserve meaningful repetition.

**5. Names and concepts**  
Use personal names, place names and names of identical institutions and objects consistently. Distinguish differing meanings or ranks according to context even when the same term is used. Reveal the identity of supernatural beings only to the extent known to the narrator or speaker.

**6. Imagery and historical values**  
Preserve East Asian imagery, exaggeration, religious worldviews and values such as filial devotion, loyalty and chastity. Do not add colours, textures or psychological details not specified in the source. Do not soften the text to conform to modern ethics or substitute Western cultural elements.

**7. Units and form**  
Do not arbitrarily convert traditional units into Western units. Preserve the established Korean edition's numbering, titles, paragraphs, event sequence, deliberate ambiguities and source inconsistencies. Use context to make the work readable without separate annotations.

**8. English spelling and expression**  
Use British spelling as the default. Conventional romanisations of Korean surnames are permitted and remain consistent within a work. Do not add intensifiers or modifiers absent from the source. Convey forms of address such as 生 and 兄 according to rank and relationships. Capitalise formal names of government offices.

**9. Magical arts and traditional concepts**  
Distinguish changes in appearance, separation of bodies, and movement between paintings and reality. Preserve the cultural significance of traditional colours, including the Five Phases, according to context. Do not adapt an ending to strengthen its literary resonance.

**10. Final comparison**  
Compare individual paragraph contents and boundaries, not just paragraph counts, with the established Korean edition. Apply corrections after review and approval and verify them again.

### 4. Shared examples of expression

- 만 번 죽어도 마땅하다: *I deserve to die ten thousand deaths.* Adjust subject and person to the source.
- 옥 같은 손을 잡았다: *took her jade-like hand*. Do not arbitrarily specify the colour, texture or softness of jade.
- 한 쌍의 원앙처럼: *like a pair of mandarin ducks*. Do not substitute Western imagery.
- 정절을 지키며 살아왔다: *I have lived in chastity.* Consider the tone of the whole sentence.
- 은자 백 냥: *one hundred nyang of silver*.
